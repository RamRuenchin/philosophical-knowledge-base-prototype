# Argument from Logical Norms Dilemma (Brown and Collins) (`ARG-19`)

## Formulation

- **Inconsistent literal set:** `¬ (P-93 ∧ P-95 ∧ ¬P-94)`

- **Asserted (`P-93`):** [[Brute Logical Error Is Possible]] — Possibly, a fully rational and cognitively blameless thinker commits an introspectively undetectable logical error.
- **Asserted (`P-95`):** [[Bridge - Brute Logical Error Requires Norm Refashioning]] — If (possibly, a fully rational and cognitively blameless thinker commits an introspectively undetectable logical error), then traditional idealized norms equating rationality with infallible a priori logical conformity must be refashioned.
- **Negated (`~P-94`):** [[Traditional Norms of Logic and Rationality Must Be Refashioned]] — It is not the case that traditional idealized norms equating rationality with infallible a priori logical conformity must be refashioned.

## Explanation

A "brute logical error" is a logical mistake—such as unwittingly equivocating between distinct concepts or holding an unnoticed contradiction—committed by a careful, competent thinker who has no way of spotting the mistake from the inside. Traditional norms of rationality treat being rational as demanding infallible armchair conformity to logic, which would unfairly convict such blameless thinkers of irrationality. If brute logical error is genuinely possible and requires revising those idealized norms, then holding on to traditional logical norms unchanged becomes impossible—forcing a choice between revising our conception of rationality and ruling out brute logical error altogether.

## Usage in Literature

### Classical Argument

[[Jessica Brown]] (`A-12`; [[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]], ch. 5, pp. 182–193) accepts the possibility of brute logical error (`P-93`, from slow-switching equivocation `ARG-01`, co-reference opacity `ARG-04`, and empirical Wason-task and Mates-puzzle cases) together with the norm-refashioning bridge (`P-95`) to reject Boghossian's idealized `B-rationality` (`D-12`) in favor of [[Blameless Identity-Sensitivity Rationality Definition]] (`D-14`, `P-26`), under which a thinker is culpable only when violating logic while acknowledging conceptual identity:

1. Possibly, a fully rational and cognitively blameless thinker commits an introspectively undetectable logical error.
2. If (possibly, a fully rational and cognitively blameless thinker commits an introspectively undetectable logical error), then traditional idealized norms equating rationality with infallible a priori logical conformity must be refashioned.
3. Therefore, traditional idealized norms equating rationality with infallible a priori logical conformity must be refashioned.

### Boghossian's Reverse Argument from Logical Norms

[[Paul Boghossian]] (`A-01`; [[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]], pp. 49–50), as explicitly formalized in [[Collins 2008 - Content Externalism and Brute Logical Error]] (p. 553), runs the same conditional in the opposite direction—arguing that excusing non-transparent contradictions collapses into triviality, and therefore ruling out brute logical error (`~P-93`) as part of a *reductio* against content externalism (`P-40`; chained with `ARG-01` and `ARG-02`):

1. It is not the case that traditional idealized norms equating rationality with infallible a priori logical conformity must be refashioned.
2. If (possibly, a fully rational and cognitively blameless thinker commits an introspectively undetectable logical error), then traditional idealized norms equating rationality with infallible a priori logical conformity must be refashioned.
3. Therefore, it is not the case that (possibly, a fully rational and cognitively blameless thinker commits an introspectively undetectable logical error).

### Premise Rejections and Replies

- **Contesting `P-95` via environmental stability and default entitlement:** [[John M. Collins]] (`A-13`; [[Collins 2008 - Content Externalism and Brute Logical Error]], pp. 552–555, 571–573) establishes `P-93` by refuting all compatibilist blocking strategies (memory shifting, concept broadening, anaphoric links, and enthymemes; see `ARG-01`) and flags [[Jessica Brown]]'s non-culpable blaming policy (`D-14`, accepting `P-94`) as plausible, while also highlighting an alternative exit that contests `P-95`: because actual physical and social environments are largely stable, thinkers may retain a default, defeasible epistemic entitlement to treat their thoughts as univocal without globally overturning traditional logical norms.
