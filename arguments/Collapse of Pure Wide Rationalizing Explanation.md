# Collapse of Pure Wide Rationalizing Explanation (`ARG-05`)

## Formulation

- **Inconsistent literal set:** `¬ (P-13 ∧ P-65 ∧ ¬P-12 ∧ P-54)`

- **Asserted (`P-13`):** [[Rational Inference Requires Comparative A Priori Transparency]] — Rational inference requires comparative a priori transparency of mental content.
- **Asserted (`P-65`):** [[Bridge - Comparative Transparency Requirement Defeats Pure Wide Rationalization]] — If both (rational inference requires comparative a priori transparency of mental content) and (it is not the case that both every thinker can know a priori, for every two same-content thought tokens of that thinker, that they have the same content, and every thinker can know a priori, for every two different-content thought tokens of that thinker, that they differ in content), then it is not the case that wide content alone suffices for rationalizing psychological explanation.
- **Negated (`~P-12`):** [[Full Comparative Epistemic Transparency Holds]] — It is not the case that both every thinker can know a priori, for every two same-content thought tokens of that thinker, that they have the same content, and every thinker can know a priori, for every two different-content thought tokens of that thinker, that they differ in content.
- **Asserted (`P-54`):** [[Wide Content Alone Suffices for Rationalizing Explanation]] — Wide content alone suffices for rationalizing psychological explanation.

## Explanation

Suppose genuine rational inference requires thinkers to know from reflection alone whenever two of their thoughts have the same meaning or different meanings. If environment-dependent ("wide") mental content fails that test—because thinkers can miss sameness or difference of wide content without realizing it—then thinkers cannot navigate logical relations purely by grasping wide contents. Those commitments therefore collide with the claim that wide content by itself is enough to explain how people reason rationally.

## Usage in Literature

### Classical Argument

[[Paul Boghossian]] (`A-01`; [[Boghossian 1992 - Externalism and Inference]], pp. 26–28; [[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]], pp. 42–46, 49–50) combines [[Rational Inference Requires Comparative A Priori Transparency]] (`P-13`) with the failure of [[Full Comparative Epistemic Transparency Holds]] (`~P-12`, derived via `¬P-11` in `ARG-02` and `¬P-10` in `ARG-04`) to refute [[Wide Content Alone Suffices for Rationalizing Explanation]] (`P-54`), motivating [[Rationalizing Explanation Requires a Non-Wide Intentional Level]] (`P-55`):

1. Rational inference requires comparative a priori transparency of mental content.
2. It is not the case that both every thinker can know a priori, for every two same-content thought tokens of that thinker, that they have the same content, and every thinker can know a priori, for every two different-content thought tokens of that thinker, that they differ in content.
3. If both (rational inference requires comparative a priori transparency of mental content) and (it is not the case that both every thinker can know a priori, for every two same-content thought tokens of that thinker, that they have the same content, and every thinker can know a priori, for every two different-content thought tokens of that thinker, that they differ in content), then it is not the case that wide content alone suffices for rationalizing psychological explanation.
4. Both (rational inference requires comparative a priori transparency of mental content) and (it is not the case that both every thinker can know a priori, for every two same-content thought tokens of that thinker, that they have the same content, and every thinker can know a priori, for every two different-content thought tokens of that thinker, that they differ in content).
5. Therefore, it is not the case that wide content alone suffices for rationalizing psychological explanation.

### Premise Rejections and Replies

- **Rejecting `P-13` (Denying the transparency requirement on rationality):** [[Jessica Brown]] (`A-12`; [[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]], pp. 182–191) and [[Stephen Schiffer]] (`A-02`; [[Schiffer 1992 - Boghossian on Externalism and Inference]], pp. 36–37) concede the failure of full comparative transparency (`~P-12`) while denying that rational inference requires it (`P-13`), defending externalist compatibilism via contingent-form (`D-13`, `P-25`) or blameless-sensitivity (`D-14`, `P-26`) accounts of rationality.
