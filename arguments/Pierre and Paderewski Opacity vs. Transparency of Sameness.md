# Pierre and Paderewski Opacity vs. Transparency of Sameness (`ARG-04`)

## Formulation

- **Inconsistent literal set:** `¬ (P-40 ∧ P-50 ∧ P-51 ∧ P-66 ∧ P-10)`

- **Asserted (`P-40`):** [[Content Externalism Holds]] — Some mental states have contents that constitutively depend on external environmental factors.
- **Asserted (`P-50`):** [[Kripke Pierre and Paderewski Co-Reference Setup Possibility]] — Possibly, a rational thinker holds two distinct co-referential mental guises without realizing that they co-refer.
- **Asserted (`P-51`):** [[Co-Referential Guise Opacity Verdict]] — If a rational thinker holds two distinct co-referential mental guises without realizing that they co-refer, then that thinker cannot know a priori that the corresponding thought tokens share wide content.
- **Asserted (`P-66`):** [[Bridge - Opaque Co-Reference Defeats Transparency of Sameness]] — If both (both (some mental states have contents that constitutively depend on external environmental factors) and (possibly, a rational thinker holds two distinct co-referential mental guises without realizing that they co-refer)) and (if a rational thinker holds two distinct co-referential mental guises without realizing that they co-refer, then that thinker cannot know a priori that the corresponding thought tokens share wide content), then it is not the case that every thinker can know a priori, for every two same-content thought tokens of that thinker, that they have the same content.
- **Asserted (`P-10`):** [[Transparency of Sameness of Content]] — Every thinker can know a priori, for every two same-content thought tokens of that thinker, that they have the same content.

## Explanation

In Kripke's Pierre and Paderewski puzzles (and similar cases like "coriander" and "cilantro"), a fully rational thinker encounters the same city, person, or plant under two separate labels without realizing they pick out the same thing. If mental content is fixed by external referents rather than fine-grained subjective guises, then those two thoughts actually share the same wide content even though the thinker cannot tell from reflection alone that they do. That directly clashes with Transparency of Sameness of Content—the claim that thinkers can always know a priori whenever two of their thoughts have the same meaning.

## Usage in Literature

### Classical Argument

[[Paul Boghossian]] (`A-01`; [[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]], pp. 37–38, 45) deploys Kripke's Pierre (`Londres`/`London`) and Loar's Paul (`chats`/`cats`) conditionally against externalism, while [[Jessica Brown]] (`A-12`; [[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]], pp. 161–166, 189–191) endorses `P-40`, `P-50`, `P-51`, and `P-66` outright under non-Fregean anti-individualism (`D-36`, using Rudolf's `coriander`/`cilantro` case) to reject [[Transparency of Sameness of Content]] (`P-10`):

1. Some mental states have contents that constitutively depend on external environmental factors.
2. Possibly, a rational thinker holds two distinct co-referential mental guises without realizing that they co-refer.
3. If a rational thinker holds two distinct co-referential mental guises without realizing that they co-refer, then that thinker cannot know a priori that the corresponding thought tokens share wide content.
4. If both (both (some mental states have contents that constitutively depend on external environmental factors) and (possibly, a rational thinker holds two distinct co-referential mental guises without realizing that they co-refer)) and (if a rational thinker holds two distinct co-referential mental guises without realizing that they co-refer, then that thinker cannot know a priori that the corresponding thought tokens share wide content), then it is not the case that every thinker can know a priori, for every two same-content thought tokens of that thinker, that they have the same content.
5. Both (some mental states have contents that constitutively depend on external environmental factors) and (possibly, a rational thinker holds two distinct co-referential mental guises without realizing that they co-refer).
6. Both (both (some mental states have contents that constitutively depend on external environmental factors) and (possibly, a rational thinker holds two distinct co-referential mental guises without realizing that they co-refer)) and (if a rational thinker holds two distinct co-referential mental guises without realizing that they co-refer, then that thinker cannot know a priori that the corresponding thought tokens share wide content).
7. Therefore, it is not the case that every thinker can know a priori, for every two same-content thought tokens of that thinker, that they have the same content.

### Premise Rejections and Replies

- **Rejecting `P-66` (Fregean anti-individualism):** As documented by [[Jessica Brown]] (`A-12`; [[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]], pp. 204–210, 226–231), Fregean anti-individualists endorsing [[Fregeanism About Mental Content]] (`P-43`) deny bridge `P-66`: because distinct co-referential guises such as `Londres` and `London` express distinct Fregean senses rather than sharing mental content, opaque co-reference does not yield two same-content thoughts and leaves `P-10` untouched (though see Morvarid Two-Concept Externalism Defeats TSC ([[Morvarid 2021 - A new argument for the incompatibility of content externalism with justification internalism#Arguments]]), for a reliability-based attack on `P-10` that still reaches Fregean two-concept externalists).
