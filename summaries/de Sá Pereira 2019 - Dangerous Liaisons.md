# de Sá Pereira 2019 - Dangerous Liaisons

**Raw source:** [[raw_sources/Pereira-2019-Dangerous Liaisons-Ratio.pdf|PDF]] · **Converted Markdown:** [[converted_markdown/Pereira-2019-Dangerous Liaisons-Ratio|Converted Markdown]]

## Abstract

In this paper I take side on externalist incompatibilism. However, I intend to radicalize the position. First, based on my criticism of Burge's anaphoric proposal, I argue that there is no reasoning‐transparency: the reasoner is blind to the reasoning he is performing. Second, assuming a global form of content‐externalism, I argue that “in the head” are only logical and formal abilities. That is what I call “bite the bullet and swallow it too.”
Source: PDF p. 1.

## Main Summary

### 1 | INTRODUCTION

Content externalism makes mental content partly depend on non-intentional environmental relations, apparently threatening a priori first-person knowledge after undetectable environmental changes. Burge's self-verifying cogito-like thoughts may preserve authoritative knowledge of present thoughts, but slow switching raises a further problem: a subject may retain an Earthian memory while newly using Twin-Earthian concepts, producing an unsafe inference. De Sá Pereira distinguishes internalist incompatibilism, externalist compatibilism, and externalist incompatibilism. He endorses and radicalizes the last: compatibilist attempts to exculpate the reasoner fail, subjects lack transparency not only about contents but about which reasoning they perform, and global externalism makes reasoning depend on rational luck. Reasoning is formally individuated by logical form but materially individuated by relations to environment and speech community. Source: PDF pp. 1–3.

### 2 | THE VIOLATION OF THE TRANSPARENCY OF SAMENESS

Boghossian's transparency of sameness says that normally functioning thinkers can introspectively know when two occurrent thoughts exercise the same concept. Kripke's Paderewski case violates this condition: Peter acquires apparently conflicting beliefs about Paderewski as musician and politician while, on a Millian and externalist account, both name-tokens express one externally individuated concept. Treating them as distinct yields a contradiction through conjunction introduction. Adding an implicit identity-denial premise does not exculpate Peter because he need not possess metaconceptual beliefs. Mental-file theory better models him as non-propositionally treating one file as two, but this only explains why he is blameless from his subjective perspective. Socially assessed, he still holds a contradiction. Because the file identity is opaque to him, he is also blind to the conjunction-introduction reasoning that he actually performs. Source: PDF pp. 3–5.

### 3 | THE VIOLATION OF TRANSPARENCY OF DIFFERENCE

Slow switching violates transparency of difference: after enough time on Twin Earth, Peter acquires TWATER without being able to know introspectively whether WATER survives alongside it, is replaced, or fuses with it. On Burge's cohabitation view, WATER and TWATER coexist, making the apparent Pavarotti inference equivocal: a generalization about twater combines with a memory about water. Its true premises do not validate its conclusion. The failure is striking because Peter is neither careless nor able to diagnose the shift a priori, but de Sá Pereira argues that this is not scandalous once rationality is socially rather than narrowly psychologically assessed. Source: PDF pp. 5–7.

### 4 | IS THERE A FALLACY OF EQUIVOCATION?

Garmendia treats the Pavarotti inference as an enthymeme with an implicit false identity premise, WATER = TWATER, thereby making it valid but unsound; Recanati similarly makes the identity a presupposed extra premise. De Sá Pereira rejects both analyses because a subject ignorant of Twin Earth cannot plausibly believe such an identity claim. Saying that Peter merely behaves as if WATER and TWATER were identical is psychologically better, but it does not remove the equivocation if Burge's cohabitation thesis is true: environmental relations still determine different semantic values that Peter cannot introspectively detect. Source: PDF pp. 7–8.

### 5 | THE ANAPHORIC SOLUTION

Burge's strongest reply reads later occurrences of “water” anaphorically to the first premise. If the first premise concerns water, all later tokens concern water and the inference is sound; if it concerns twater, all later tokens concern twater and the inference is valid but unsound because the remembered event never occurred. De Sá Pereira grants that anaphora may preserve content and avoid equivocation. Yet the two readings are different reasonings—one sound and one unsound—while the thinker cannot know which he performs. Thus Burge's proposal deepens, rather than defeats, skepticism about reasoning-transparency: if meaning is not in the head, reasoning is not either. Source: PDF pp. 8–9.

### 6 | THE REPLACEMENT OF CONCEPTS

Tye's presentist replacement view holds that TWATER entirely replaces WATER, so every occurrence in the remembered inference refers to Twin-Earthian items. This avoids equivocation but makes the first premise false: Peter never witnessed Twin-Pavarotti floating on twater. The view may subjectively excuse Peter, but it does not show that his reasoning is objectively felicitous. De Sá Pereira rejects the claim that the present environment determines the content of the past memory and maintains that reasoning is partly individuated by the multiple environments involved. Source: PDF p. 9.

### 7 | THE FUSION OF FILES

Recanati's mental-file framework supports a fusion strategy: the separate WATER and TWATER files merge into WATER-AND-TWATER because Peter ignores the switch. The resulting inference is valid but unsound, since Pavarotti never floated on the fused stuff. As with replacement, this may explain subjective blamelessness but does not secure objective success. Source: PDF pp. 9–10.

### 8 | INTRINSIC AND EXTRINSIC FALLACIES

Sorensen distinguishes intrinsic equivocation, caused by mishandling meanings available within a speaker's psychology, from extrinsic equivocation produced by environmental variation. He compares Twin-Earthable expressions to indexicals. De Sá Pereira objects that natural-kind terms are not indexicals: their meaning changes with reference, they are not context-to-content rules, and they have contents rather than mere content-schemas. Boghossian could therefore exclude genuine indexicals from transparency while retaining his challenge about WATER and TWATER. More fundamentally, Sorensen's distinction presupposes local externalism. Under global externalism, every concept is partly individuated by environmental and communal relations, so there is no principled boundary between intrinsic and extrinsic equivocation. A slow-switching case involving two communities' different uses of “end” reproduces a supposedly intrinsic textbook fallacy as an extrinsic one. Source: PDF pp. 10–12.

### 9 | CONCLUSION

De Sá Pereira first challenges comparative-content transparency independently of externalism: introspection is not perceptual discrimination, and even an internalist may be unable to tell whether two qualitatively identical experiences generate one demonstrative concept or two. He then draws the global-externalist conclusion. Paderewski-Peter and Switched-Peter may be blameless internally, yet their reasonings are socially assessed as contradictory, invalid, or unsound because semantic values depend on relations beyond their control. Successful inference therefore requires more than an internal ability to follow a formal rule. Environment and speech community help individuate the reasoning itself, so only formal abilities are wholly “in the head” while reasoning is both formally and materially individuated. Source: PDF p. 12.
