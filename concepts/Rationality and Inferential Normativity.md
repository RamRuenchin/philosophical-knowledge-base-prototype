# Rationality and Inferential Normativity (`C-03`)

## Formulation

`Rationality and Inferential Normativity` is the normative standard by which a thinker's inferences, belief revisions, and transitions in thought are evaluated as rational, warranted, or rationally intelligible in psychological explanation.

## Explanation

This concept concerns the standards that make a person's reasoning rational, justified, or intelligible to others. It becomes a major battleground under [[Content Externalism]] because a thinker who is secretly moved between two look-alike environments can draw an inference that looks completely flawless from the inside while actually committing a hidden fallacy of equivocation because the same word quietly refers to two different substances or people. Philosophers disagree over whether genuine rationality requires you to be able to rule out such hidden logical errors from the armchair, or whether you can still count as rational when you exercise reliable everyday reasoning habits and are simply unlucky about your environment.

### Candidate Sharpenings
- [[A Priori Logical-Conformity Rationality Definition]] (`D-12`) — Rational inference and rational psychological explanation require the thinker to be able to track the actual, world-dependent logical relations among her thoughts purely from the armchair.
- [[Contingent-Form Rationality Definition]] (`D-13`) — A deduction can count as rational and justified as long as its premises happen in fact to share the same world-dependent meanings, even if the thinker cannot guarantee that sameness from the armchair.
- [[Blameless Identity-Sensitivity Rationality Definition]] (`D-14`) — Rationality requires being blamelessly sensitive to logical structure in a way that still allows a thinker to make an undetectable, non-culpable logical error in abnormal externalist circumstances.
- [[Competence-Based Normal-Environment Rationality Definition]] (`D-15`) — Rationality consists in exercising a fallible reasoning skill that reliably yields valid inferences in normal environments, even when an unusual environment trips it up.
- [[Logical-Luck Externalist Rationality Definition]] (`D-16`) — Rationality in reasoning accepts a form of "logical luck," where whether a concrete inference succeeds depends partly on cooperating background facts in the external environment.
- [[Dual Internal-Blamelessness and Social-Material Rationality Definition]] (`D-17`) — Rational evaluation splits into two distinct standards: whether the thinker is blameless from her own internal viewpoint, and whether her inference is valid given socially and environmentally fixed meanings.

## Usage in Literature

[[Paul Boghossian]] frames the challenge around a priori logical-conformity rationality (`D-12`), arguing that if wide contents can equivocate undetectably in Peter's inference, wide content alone cannot sustain rationalizing psychological explanation and must be supplemented by a non-wide level ([[Boghossian 1992 - Externalism and Inference]]; [[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]]; [[Collapse of Pure Wide Rationalizing Explanation]]). [[Tyler Burge]] accepts a demanding logical-conformity picture (`D-12`) but defends externalist compatibility by holding that preservative memory secures univocality in Peter's deduction, whereas [[Stephen Schiffer]] both invokes univocal referential intent and relaxes rationality to contingent-form justification (`D-13`) ([[Schiffer 1992 - Boghossian on Externalism and Inference]]; [[Boghossian 1992 - Reply to Schiffer]]).

Later externalists largely concede that slow switching produces undetectable equivocation and therefore refashion inferential normativity rather than blocking the equivocation. [[Jessica Brown]] and [[John M. Collins]] diagnose the *Argument from Logical Norms*—showing that externalism permits *brute logical error* without cognitive fault—with Brown explicitly endorsing blameless identity-sensitivity (`D-14`) and calling for refashioned logical norms, while Collins leaves open which externalist revision is best ([[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]]; [[Collins 2008 - Content Externalism and Brute Logical Error]]; [[Argument from Logical Norms Dilemma (Brown and Collins)]]). [[Mikkel Gerken]] rejects mere deontological blamelessness via the gambler's fallacy case and replaces it with competence-based normal-environment rationality (`D-15`), on which fallible reasoning competencies still confer conditional warrant in abnormal slow-switch environments ([[Gerken 2011 - Conceptual Equivocation and Warrant by Reasoning]]). [[Paulo Faria]] and [[Manuel Pérez Otero]] highlight the parallel with moral luck, exploring logical-luck externalist normativity (`D-16`) in which empirical co-reference conditions act as enabling presuppositions rather than premises ([[Faria 2009 - Unsafe reasoning A survey]]; [[Otero 2012 - Boghossian's Inference Argument against Content Externalism Reversed]]). Finally, [[Roberto Horácio de Sá Pereira]] separates internal blamelessness from socially and materially individuated validity (`D-17`), arguing that global externalism makes reasoning itself materially individuated ([[de Sá Pereira 2019 - Dangerous Liaisons]]), while [[Brie Gertler]] links rational inference to first-order rational sensitivity rather than reflective access ([[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism]]).
