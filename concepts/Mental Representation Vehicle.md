# Mental Representation Vehicle (`C-13`)

## Formulation

`Mental Representation Vehicle` is the cognitive architecture or semantic modeling unit posited to represent propositional attitudes, sub-propositional concepts, and co-reference relations across a thinker's reasoning and belief revision.

## Explanation

If a thought's *content* is what it says about the world, a *mental representation vehicle* is the psychological structure or modeling unit the mind uses to carry and organize that content across beliefs and inferences. Philosophers in the externalism debate fight over whether we need structured, concept-sized mental vehicles—such as "mental files" that bundle information about a single object together—to explain how people track sameness of topic and rationally revise beliefs over time. Critics of internal mental structure argue that we can model beliefs more simply as coarse-grained sets of possible worlds (all the ways the world could be if the belief were true) without positing hidden mental files or internal concepts at all.

### Candidate Sharpenings
- [[Minimalist Person-Level Mental Files Definition]] (`D-42`) — Lightweight mental "files" that group a thinker's attitudes together so she treats them directly as being about the same object, without guaranteeing infallible armchair knowledge of what they mean.
- [[Unstructured Possible-Worlds Propositions Definition]] (`D-43`) — Beliefs are modeled purely as unstructured sets of possible worlds representing how things might be, doing away with sub-propositional concepts or mental files altogether.

## Usage in Literature

[[Robert Stalnaker]] advances unstructured possible-worlds propositions (`D-43`) as part of his thoroughly externalist, anti-Cartesian alternative to what [[Paul Boghossian]] calls the *Common View* ([[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]]; [[Schroeter 2013 - Are Concepts Creatures of Darkness]]). On Stalnaker's model, eliminating Fregean senses and sub-propositional concepts avoids mysterious internal "creatures of darkness," and puzzles like Kripke's Pierre or Boghossian's Peter are handled by interpreter-relative possible-worlds attributions and tacit contextual presuppositions ([[Unstructured Possible-Worlds Propositions Suffice Without Concepts]]).

[[Laura Schroeter]] challenges `D-43` directly, arguing that Stalnaker's unstructured possible-worlds framework—paired with radical interpreter-relative contextualism (`P-33`)—cannot explain normative diachronic belief revision or the immediate person-level phenomenon of *apparent de jure co-reference* across a thinker's attitudes ([[Schroeter 2013 - Are Concepts Creatures of Darkness]]; Schroeter Normative Belief Revision Argument Against Radical Contextualism ([[Schroeter 2013 - Are Concepts Creatures of Darkness#Arguments]])). In its place, Schroeter defends minimalist person-level mental files (`D-42`): functional vehicles that bind and segregate a thinker's changing bodies of information and immediately dispose her to treat bound attitudes as co-referential without guaranteeing infallible Cartesian transparency (Externalist Semantics Requires Minimalist Mental Files ([[Schroeter 2013 - Are Concepts Creatures of Darkness#Propositions]])). In a parallel vein, [[Paul Boghossian]] emphasizes that narrow syntactic or representational-vehicle identity (as in a language of thought or psychological modes) remains indispensable for explaining Peter's rational preparedness when wide contents diverge ([[Boghossian 1992 - Externalism and Inference]]; [[Boghossian 2014 - Reply to Otero's Boghossian's Inference Argument against Content Externalism Reversed]]).
