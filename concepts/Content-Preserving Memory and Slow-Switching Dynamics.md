# Content-Preserving Memory and Slow-Switching Dynamics (`C-05`)

## Formulation

`Content-Preserving Memory and Slow-Switching Dynamics` is the semantic and cognitive mechanism governing how a thinker's standing concepts and memorial beliefs are retained, updated, or co-referenced after an unnoticed relocation between physically or socially distinct environments (slow switching).

## Explanation

In the classic "slow-switching" thought experiment, someone is secretly moved from Earth to an indistinguishable Twin Earth (where the watery liquid has a different chemical formula, or where a look-alike person shares the same name) and lives there long enough to pick up the local meanings. How memory behaves after such a switch is decisive for [[Content Externalism]]: if your memory preserves an old Earth-based belief alongside a newly formed Twin-Earth belief under the same word, combining them in a deduction silently mixes two different concepts without your noticing. Philosophers therefore fight over what actually happens to remembered concepts after an unnoticed move—whether old and new concepts coexist side by side, whether reasoning automatically locks all uses of a word onto one meaning, whether the new concept overwrites the old, or whether the two merge into a single hybrid concept.

### Candidate Sharpenings
- [[Two-Concept Originary Fixation Memory Definition]] (`D-21`) — After a slow switch, memory keeps the original Earth concept intact while new experience adds a separate Twin-Earth concept, allowing undetectable equivocation when both enter one inference.
- [[Anaphoric Univocality-Preserving Memory Definition]] (`D-22`) — Memory and the thinker's intention to use a term consistently bind every occurrence of the word in a single inference to the same meaning, preventing equivocation.
- [[Presentist Concept-Displacement Memory Definition]] (`D-23`) — After a slow switch, the newly acquired Twin-Earth concept completely replaces the old Earth concept throughout memory, leaving only one concept behind.
- [[Amalgam File-Fusion Memory Definition]] (`D-24`) — After a slow switch, the Earth and Twin-Earth concepts fuse into a single blended or disjunctive concept covering both.

## Usage in Literature

[[Paul Boghossian]] inaugurates the slow-switching dialectic by arguing that after Peter unknowingly moves from Earth to Twin Earth, memory preserves his originary Earthly belief (`Pavarotti` / `water`) while current interactions generate a distinct Twin-Earth concept (`D-21`), causing Peter's superficially valid syllogism to equivocate undetectably ([[Boghossian 1992 - Externalism and Inference]]; [[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]]; [[Slow-Switching Equivocation Derivation]]). Boghossian explicitly rejects presentist concept displacement (`D-23`) as implausibly falsifying ordinary historical memories. [[Stephen Schiffer]] and [[Tyler Burge]] counter with anaphoric univocality-preserving memory (`D-22`), arguing that a thinker's standing intention to reuse a non-indexical term univocally ties the premises of a deduction to the same semantic value and blocks equivocation ([[Schiffer 1992 - Boghossian on Externalism and Inference]]; [[Schiffer Univocal Intent Blocks Slow-Switch Equivocation]]). Boghossian replies that anaphoric links cannot prevent equivocation when a thinker draws two independent memory beliefs acquired in different environments into a single inference ([[Boghossian 1992 - Reply to Schiffer]]).

Subsequent literature systematically audits all four models. [[Jessica Brown]] and [[John M. Collins]] evaluate and reject displacement (`D-23`), amalgam file-fusion (`D-24`), and universal anaphoric blocking (`D-22`), concluding that anti-individualists should accept two-concept cohabitation (`D-21`) and slow-switch equivocation while revising logical norms instead ([[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]]; [[Collins 2008 - Content Externalism and Brute Logical Error]]). [[Sanford C. Goldberg]] turns the clash between `D-21` and `D-22` into a dilemma for Discursive Justification ([[Goldberg-2007-Anti-Individualism, Content Preservation, and Discursive Justification#Concepts]]): either memorial preservation holds (`D-21`) and deduction fails by equivocation, or anaphoric univocality holds (`D-22`) and memorial content preservation fails ([[Goldberg-2007-Anti-Individualism, Content Preservation, and Discursive Justification]]). Both [[Mikkel Gerken]] and [[Mahmoud Morvarid]] build their core arguments directly on two-concept cohabitation (`D-21`), whereas [[Manuel Pérez Otero]] treats anaphora (`D-22`) as one viable externalist option, [[Henry Jackman]] derives univocality without `D-22` via single-context commitment weighting, and [[Roberto Horácio de Sá Pereira]] critiques `D-22`, `D-23`, and `D-24` alike before accepting equivocation as a feature of materially individuated reasoning ([[Gerken 2011 - Conceptual Equivocation and Warrant by Reasoning]]; [[Morvarid 2021 - A new argument for the incompatibility of content externalism with justification internalism]]; [[Otero 2012 - Boghossian's Inference Argument against Content Externalism Reversed]]; [[Jackman 2015 - Externalism, metasemantic contextualism, and self-knowledge]]; [[de Sá Pereira 2019 - Dangerous Liaisons]]).
