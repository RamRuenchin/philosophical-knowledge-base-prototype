# Knowledge of Content (`C-06`)

## Formulation

`Knowledge of Content` is the pre-theoretic phenomenon of first-person introspective epistemic access to what one is thinking—the distinctive, non-empirical self-knowledge thinkers ordinarily claim regarding the intentional contents of their own thoughts.

## Explanation

This concept captures the everyday idea that you have a special, direct kind of self-knowledge of what you are thinking, without needing to observe your own behavior or run scientific tests. Philosophers fight over whether [[Content Externalism]] undermines this self-knowledge: if what your thought means depends on your outside environment, can you really know what you are thinking just from the inside? Much of the clash comes down to splitting two very different abilities—simply knowing what a single thought means as you think it, versus being able to tell from the inside whether two thoughts have the same meaning or whether your current thought is about Earthly water rather than an indistinguishable twin liquid.

### Candidate Sharpenings
- [[Knowledge of Content Proper Definition]] (`D-25`) — Direct, authoritative first-person knowledge of what a single current thought means (such as knowing that you are thinking that water is wet), without needing to distinguish it from twin alternatives.
- [[Discriminatory Knowledge of Content Definition]] (`D-26`) — The introspective ability to tell whether two of your thoughts share or differ in meaning, or to distinguish your thought's actual meaning from relevant look-alike alternatives.

## Usage in Literature

The distinction between `KC` (`D-25`) and `DKC` (`D-26`) drives the compatibilist response to slow-switching skepticism. [[Tyler Burge]] and [[Jessica Brown]] defend compatibilism by arguing that privileged access requires only `KC` (`D-25`): a thinker can know authoritatively and a priori that she is thinking that *water is wet*—since the second-order self-ascription inherits the first-order thought's external content—even if she cannot discriminate *water* thoughts from *twater* thoughts (`D-26`) without empirical investigation ([[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]]; [[Goldberg 1999 - The Relevance of Discriminatory Knowledge of Content]]). [[Paul Boghossian]] grants that anti-individualism can secure single-token *cogito*-style `KC` (`D-25`) while insisting that reasoning requires comparative discriminative access ([[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]]), and [[Brie Gertler]] likewise accepts `KC` compatibilism while contesting its significance for internalism about [[Cognitive Value]] ([[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism]]).

[[Sanford C. Goldberg]] challenges the compatibilist dismissal of `DKC` (`D-26`) as epistemically irrelevant. Through his pragmatist Sally thought experiment (Sally DKC Relevance Argument ([[Goldberg 1999 - The Relevance of Discriminatory Knowledge of Content#Arguments]])), Goldberg argues that when a thinker's standing anti-individualist beliefs leave her uncertain whether two remembered or occurrent thought tokens are univocal, her lack of `DKC` directly impedes truth-directed deductive reasoning—even if she retains `KC` for each individual thought and intends to use her terms univocally ([[Goldberg 1999 - The Relevance of Discriminatory Knowledge of Content]]). [[John M. Collins]] and [[Mahmoud Morvarid]] draw closely related distinctions between authoritative single-thought self-knowledge and comparative discriminative self-knowledge when diagnosing brute logical error and the tension between externalism and [[Justification Internalism]] ([[Collins 2008 - Content Externalism and Brute Logical Error]]; [[Morvarid 2021 - A new argument for the incompatibility of content externalism with justification internalism]]).
