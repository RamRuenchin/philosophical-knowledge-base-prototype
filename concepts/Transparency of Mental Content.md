# Transparency of Mental Content (`C-01`)

## Formulation

`Transparency of Mental Content` is the pre-theoretic epistemic openness of a thinker's own thought contents—including whether two of her thoughts share or differ in content—to first-person reflection or reasoning without empirical investigation.

## Explanation

Ordinarily, we assume our own minds are open to us: just by reflecting from the armchair, without investigating the outside world, you can tell what you are thinking and whether two of your thoughts mean the same thing or different things. Philosophers fight over this idea because if the meaning of your thoughts depends on your external physical or social environment ([[Content Externalism]]), then secretly switching environments—or encountering the same person or substance in two different guises—could make two thoughts differ in meaning (or match in meaning) without your being able to tell from the inside. Much of the debate turns on how strong this "transparency" actually needs to be for self-knowledge and rational reasoning to work; note also that this inner-access notion is completely separate from the tool-use notion of Phenomenal Transparency ([[Hauser-Naeem-2024-Phenomenal Transparency and the Boundary of Cognition#Concepts]]) in extended cognition.

### Candidate Sharpenings
- [[Comparative A Priori Transparency Definition]] (`D-01`) — You can tell purely by reflection, without empirical inquiry, both whenever two of your thoughts share the same meaning and whenever they differ in meaning.
- [[Content Infallibility Definition]] (`D-02`) — You have authoritative, error-free knowledge of what a single current thought means simply by thinking it, even if you cannot compare two thoughts to tell whether their meanings match.
- [[Qualified Contextual Transparency Definition]] (`D-03`) — Your access to your own thought meanings holds only relative to your background assumptions and how an interpreter assigns meanings in a given conversational context.
- [[Modest Synchronic Transparency Definition]] (`D-04`) — You have a modest, fallible grasp of what your concepts mean at a single moment that fits with externalism, without guaranteeing you can always track whether a concept stays the same over time.
- [[Strong Meaning Rationalism Definition]] (`D-05`) — You have an infallible, purely armchair grasp of whether any of your concepts are identical, distinct, or actually refer to anything in reality.
- [[First-Order Rational Sensitivity Definition]] (`D-06`) — Your reasoning and belief-updating automatically respond appropriately to how your thoughts relate in meaning, even if you cannot step back and state those meaning relations explicitly.
- [[Reflective Explicatory Access Definition]] (`D-07`) — You can step back and explicitly articulate, from the armchair alone, what makes your concepts apply and why your inferences are justified.
- [[Reasoning-Transparency Definition]] (`D-08`) — You can always tell from the inside which formal pattern of reasoning you are performing, regardless of how external facts determine the meanings of your terms.

## Usage in Literature

[[Paul Boghossian]] anchors the contemporary debate by separating single-token content infallibility (`D-02`)—which he concedes externalism can accommodate via [[Tyler Burge]]'s *cogito*-style self-verifying judgments—from comparative transparency (`D-01`), comprising Transparency of Sameness (`TSC`, [[Transparency of Sameness of Content]]) and Transparency of Difference (`TDC`, [[Transparency of Difference of Content]]) ([[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]]; [[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]]). Deploying Kripke's Pierre/Paderewski cases against `TSC` and slow-switching (Peter) against `TDC`, Boghossian contends that externalism violates `D-01` and thereby imperils rational explanation. Subsequent externalists split sharply on whether to reject `D-01` outright or replace it with a weaker notion: [[Jessica Brown]] and [[John M. Collins]] accept `D-01` as the relevant notion of transparency, endorse Boghossian's case that externalism defeats it—Brown adding the non-switch Frances case against Fregean `TDC`—and deny that rationality requires it ([[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]]; [[Collins 2008 - Content Externalism and Brute Logical Error]]). [[Sanford C. Goldberg]] likewise rejects `TDC` and shows via his Schmidt case that Frege's differential-dubitability test does not presuppose it ([[Goldberg-2008-Must Differences in Cognitive Value be Transparent]]), while [[Mahmoud Morvarid]] uses slow-switching unreliability against `TSC` to derive an incompatibility between externalism and [[Justification Internalism]] ([[Morvarid 2021 - A new argument for the incompatibility of content externalism with justification internalism]]).

Other contributors reshape the concept itself. [[Robert Stalnaker]] rejects Fregean intentional realism to defend qualified contextual transparency (`D-03`), which Boghossian criticizes as viciously circular ([[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]]). In response to [[Ruth Garrett Millikan]]'s blanket attack on Strong Meaning Rationalism (`D-05`), [[Tadeusz Szubka]] salvages modest synchronic transparency (`D-04`) as externalism-friendly ([[Szubka 2000 - Meaning Rationalism, A Priori, and Transparency of Content]]). [[Brie Gertler]] separates first-order rational sensitivity (`D-06`), which she holds rational inference requires, from reflective explicatory access (`D-07`), which she refutes via Shoemaker's Rita case ([[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism]]). Radicalizing incompatibilism from the externalist side, [[Roberto Horácio de Sá Pereira]] targets reasoning-transparency (`D-08`), arguing that global externalism materially individuates reasoning and leaves thinkers blind even to which inference they perform ([[de Sá Pereira 2019 - Dangerous Liaisons]]).
