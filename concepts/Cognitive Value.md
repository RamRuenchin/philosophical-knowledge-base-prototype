# Cognitive Value (`C-10`)

## Formulation

`Cognitive Value` is the Fregean or epistemic significance of a thought—the aspect of mental content that accounts for how an attitude figures in rational coherence, belief revision, differential dubitability (Frege's puzzle), and rationalizing psychological explanation.

## Explanation

Following Gottlob Frege's classic puzzle about why "Hesperus is Phosphorus" is informative even though both names refer to Venus, *cognitive value* is the aspect of a thought's meaning that explains how it behaves in rational thought—for example, why a rational person can believe one thought while doubting another that refers to the very same object. Philosophers fight over cognitive value because it pulls in opposite directions under [[Content Externalism]]: on one side, two physically identical twins seem equally rational from the inside, suggesting cognitive value is purely internal; on the other side, externalists argue that public language and deferring to one's community help fix how we think of things, or even that Fregean "modes of presentation" should be dropped altogether.

### Candidate Sharpenings
- [[Narrow Fregean Cognitive Value Definition]] (`D-34`) — Cognitive value is a purely internal way of thinking about an object that depends solely on what is inside the thinker's mind and governs her rational coherence.
- [[Social-Externalist Public Cognitive Value Definition]] (`D-35`) — Cognitive value is a Fregean way of thinking that is partly shaped by public linguistic norms, community usage, and the thinker's deference to other speakers.
- [[Non-Fregean Wide Content Individuation Definition]] (`D-36`) — Mental content does without Fregean ways of thinking altogether, relying instead on environmentally determined ("wide") contents alone.

## Usage in Literature

The debate over `Cognitive Value` centers on whether Frege's *differential dubitability test*—two thoughts differ in cognitive value if a rational thinker can believe one while doubting the other—forces content internalism (`D-34`) or can be reconciled with [[Content Externalism]] (`D-35`, `D-36`). [[Brie Gertler]] defends narrow Fregean cognitive value (`D-34`): setting aside higher-order accessibilism, she deploys the Differential Dubitability biconditional in [[Tyler Burge]]'s arthritis case (Bert) to argue that because Bert and his counterfactual twin exhibit identical rational coherence profiles, cognitive value must supervene on internal mental states ([[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism]]; Differential Dubitability Argument for Narrow Cognitive Value ([[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism#Arguments]])). By contrast, [[Paul Boghossian]] treats Fregeanism about mental content (combined with intentional realism as the *Common View*) as threatened by externalism precisely because opaque co-reference (Pierre/Paderewski) and slow switching sever wide content from transparent rationalizing significance ([[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]]; [[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]]).

Externalists divide into Fregean and non-Fregean camps. [[Sanford C. Goldberg]], drawing on [[Tyler Burge]], defends social-externalist public cognitive value (`D-35`): using his slow-switched Schmidt case, Goldberg shows that a rational thinker can differentially doubt two thoughts by identifying them via episode-relative demonstrative or memorial routes without introspectively discriminating their contents—refuting the assumption that Frege's Test requires [[Transparency of Difference of Content]] (`TDC`)—and argues that semantic deference makes public linguistic standards constitutive of cognitive value ([[Goldberg-2008-Must Differences in Cognitive Value be Transparent]]; Schmidt Differential Dubitability Without TDC ([[Goldberg-2008-Must Differences in Cognitive Value be Transparent#Arguments]])). [[Jessica Brown]], however, rejects Fregean anti-individualism (`D-35`) altogether in favor of non-Fregean wide content (`D-36`), arguing via her non-switch *Frances* case that even Fregean anti-individualists cannot preserve `TDC` and should instead explain Frege cases without Fregean modes of presentation ([[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]]; Frances Element Case Refutes Fregean Transparency of Difference ([[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge#Arguments]])).
