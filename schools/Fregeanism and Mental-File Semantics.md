# Fregeanism and Mental-File Semantics (`S-04`)

## Guiding Idea

`Fregeanism and Mental-File Semantics` is the semantic and cognitive-architecture research program that explains cognitive significance, rational coherence, belief revision, and apparent *de jure* co-reference by positing fine-grained modes of presentation, concepts, or person-level mental files rather than bare external referents or unstructured sets of possible worlds. Across the slow-switching and transparency literature, the program divides into internalist/narrow Fregeanism, social-externalist Fregeanism, and minimalist mental-file semantics, united by the conviction that rational thought requires structured representational intermediaries beyond coarse truth-conditions.

## Core Apparatus

### Core Propositions and Theories
- [[Fregeanism About Mental Content]] (`P-43`) — Mental contents are individuated by Fregean modes of presentation rather than solely by external objects and properties.
- [[The Common View of Mental Content]] (`P-44`) — Conjoins [[Intentional Realism]] (`P-42`) with [[Fregeanism About Mental Content]] (`P-43`) as the standard background framework challenged by Stalnaker.
- [[Public Linguistic Standards Partly Determine Cognitive Value]] (`P-79`) — Social-externalist Fregean thesis holding that shared public linguistic norms partly fix cognitive value to sustain testimonial transmission.
- [[Unstructured Possible-Worlds Propositions Suffice Without Concepts]] (`P-90`) — Primary anti-representationalist foil rejecting Fregean senses and minimalist mental files in favor of unstructured sets of possible worlds (`D-43`).

### Core Concepts
- [[Cognitive Value]] (`C-10`) — Captures the aspect of mental content that enters rational deliberation, differential doubt, and inference (sharpened into narrow Fregean `D-34`, social-externalist public `D-35`, and non-Fregean wide `D-36`).
- [[Mental Representation Vehicle]] (`C-13`) — Contrasts minimalist person-level mental files (`D-42`) with unstructured possible-worlds propositions (`D-43`) as the underlying architecture of thought.

### Core Arguments and Key Single-Source Arguments
- [[Pierre and Paderewski Opacity vs. Transparency of Sameness]] (`ARG-04`) — Tests whether unrecognized co-reference under distinct modes of presentation or mental files defeats transparency of sameness (`P-10`).
- [[Schroeter 2013 - Are Concepts Creatures of Darkness#Arguments]] — Refutes Stalnaker's anti-representationalist possible-worlds picture (`P-90`) via normative diachronic belief revision, motivating minimalist person-level mental files (`D-42`) that encode apparent *de jure* co-reference.
- [[Goldberg-2008-Must Differences in Cognitive Value be Transparent#Arguments]] — Defends social-externalist public cognitive value (`P-79`, `D-35`) via testimonial transmission while using slow-switched Schmidt to argue that cognitive value differences need not satisfy transparency of difference (`P-11`).
- See also [[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism#Arguments]] (narrow Fregean defense of cognitive value via Differential Dubitability) and [[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge#Arguments]] (Frances element case arguing that even neo-Fregean anti-individualism fails transparency of difference).

## Development Across Literature

Gottlob Frege's distinction between reference (*Bedeutung*) and sense/cognitive value (*Sinn*) became the centerpiece of what [[Paul Boghossian]] calls [[The Common View of Mental Content]] (`P-44`; [[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]]). Because Saul Kripke's Pierre and Paderewski cases (`ARG-04`) showed that rational thinkers can hold conflicting attitudes toward the same referent under distinct guises, philosophers debated whether [[Content Externalism]] (`S-01`) can coherently pair environmental reference with Fregean modes of presentation—or whether externalist senses themselves violate transparency.

Three major developments in this corpus shape the contemporary program:
- **Externalist vs. non-Fregean critiques:** [[Jessica Brown]] ([[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]]) deployed her non-switched Frances element case to argue that even neo-Fregean anti-individualism violates [[Transparency of Difference of Content]] (`P-11`), leading her to reject Fregeanism (`P-43`) in favor of non-Fregean wide content (`D-36`). By contrast, [[Sanford C. Goldberg]] ([[Goldberg-2008-Must Differences in Cognitive Value be Transparent]]) and [[Tyler Burge]] retained externalist [[Cognitive Value]] grounded in shared public linguistic standards (`P-79`, `D-35`), conceding that cognitive value differences need not be introspectively transparent.
- **Narrow Fregeanism without accessibilism:** [[Brie Gertler]] ([[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism]]) rejected `P-79` and defended narrow Fregean cognitive value (`D-34`) via a qualified Differential Dubitability biconditional, arguing that deference to public authorities is itself an internal first-order disposition and requires no higher-order explicatory access.
- **From Fregean senses to minimalist mental files:** Confronting [[Robert Stalnaker]]'s elimination of concepts as Cartesian "creatures of darkness" (`P-90`), [[Laura Schroeter]] ([[Schroeter 2013 - Are Concepts Creatures of Darkness]]) rehabilitated structured representational vehicles in a minimalist externalist key: person-level mental files (`D-42`) need not carry infallible Cartesian descriptions, yet remain indispensable to represent internal mind–mind links (apparent *de jure* co-reference) across rational belief revision.
