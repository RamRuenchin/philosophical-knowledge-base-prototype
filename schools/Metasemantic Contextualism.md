# Metasemantic Contextualism (`S-03`)

## Guiding Idea

`Metasemantic Contextualism` is the metasemantic methodology according to which the underlying content-determination function—fixing what a thinker's standing thoughts and terms mean in the first place, not merely evaluating built-in indexicals—varies across contexts of deliberation, attribution, or historical anchoring. Developed primarily to rescue [[Content Externalism]] (`S-01`) from slow-switching equivocation and transparency puzzles, it treats context-sensitive content assignment as a way to keep a thinker's reasoning coherent across environmental switches without retreating to narrow internalism.

## Core Apparatus

### Core Propositions and Theories
- [[Metasemantic Contextualism Adequately Explains Mental Content]] (`P-06`) — Umbrella thesis directly sharpened into the three competing contextualist proposals below (`P-32`, `P-33`, `P-34`).
- [[Speaker-Weighting Metasemantic Contextualism Holds]] (`P-32`) — Context shifts the relative weights of the speaker's own commitments under the Self-Interpretation Principle, keeping repeated terms univocal within any single deliberative context.
- [[Radical Interpreter-Relative Contextualism Holds]] (`P-33`) — Standing beliefs have no context-neutral representational contents; their truth conditions shift with an outside interpreter's conversational spot-check and explanatory aims.
- [[Source-Dependent Contextualism Holds]] (`P-34`) — Each individual memory or premise token has its referent fixed separately by the causal-historical acquisition episode of that specific belief.
- [[Contextualist Content Externalism]] (`P-118`) — Externalist theory pairing environmental individuation with context-variable content assignment across switch episodes.

### Core Concepts
- [[Content-Preserving Memory and Slow-Switching Dynamics]] (`C-05`) — Supplies the slow-switching memory cases (`Peter`, `water`/`twater`) that metasemantic contextualism aims to reinterpret without equivocation.
- [[Mental Representation Vehicle]] (`C-13`) — Marks the clash between anti-representationalist possible-worlds contextualism (`P-33`, `P-90`) and stable person-level mental files (`D-42`).

### Key Single-Source Arguments
- [[Jackman 2015 - Externalism, metasemantic contextualism, and self-knowledge#Arguments]] — Uses the Self-Interpretation Principle and variable commitment weights to argue that because slow-switched Peter's inference occupies one deliberative context, uniform weighting forces univocality and refutes equivocation (`P-47`).
- [[Schroeter 2013 - Are Concepts Creatures of Darkness#Arguments]] — Argues that radical interpreter-relative contextualism (`P-33`) undermines normative diachronic belief revision, requiring stable person-level mental files instead.
- [[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited#Arguments]] — Attacks Stalnaker's tacit identity presupposition strategy as circular and argues that radical attributor-relativity triggers an attributional regress unless [[Intentional Realism]] (`P-42`) holds for some contents.

## Development Across Literature

[[Robert Stalnaker]] initiated the contextualist turn in slow-switching debates by rejecting [[The Common View of Mental Content]] (`P-44` — both [[Intentional Realism]], `P-42`, and [[Fregeanism About Mental Content]], `P-43`) in favor of radical interpreter-relative contextualism (`P-33`) and unstructured possible-worlds propositions (`P-90`), using conversational "spot-checks" and tacit identity presuppositions to dissolve Kripke's Pierre and Boghossian's Peter puzzles without concepts (reconstructed in [[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]] and [[Schroeter 2013 - Are Concepts Creatures of Darkness]]).

Both [[Paul Boghossian]] and [[Laura Schroeter]] mounted foundational objections to `P-33`: Boghossian ([[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]]) argued that Stalnaker's tacit-presupposition criterion is viciously circular and generates an infinite attributional regress unless some contents are fixed independently of interpreters, while Schroeter ([[Schroeter 2013 - Are Concepts Creatures of Darkness]]) showed that interpreter-relative truth conditions destabilize normative belief revision across time.

[[Henry Jackman]] ([[Jackman 2015 - Externalism, metasemantic contextualism, and self-knowledge]]) sought to rehabilitate the program by replacing Stalnaker's interpreter-relative variant (`P-33`)—and diagnosing Boghossian's originary-fixation reading of Peter as an unmotivated source-dependent contextualism (`P-34`)—with speaker-weighting metasemantic contextualism (`P-32`). Grounded in semantic holism and the Self-Interpretation Principle, `P-32` anchors contextual variation to the thinker's own shifting commitment priorities so that all premises within a single surveyable inference receive a uniform semantic assignment, blocking slow-switch equivocation while preserving externalism (`P-40`, `P-118`).
