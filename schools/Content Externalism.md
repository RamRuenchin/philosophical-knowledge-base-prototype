# Content Externalism (`S-01`)

## Guiding Idea

`Content Externalism` (also called *semantic anti-individualism*) is the broad metasemantic research program holding that what a thinker's intentional mental states are about does not supervene solely on intrinsic, non-relational neurophysiological or phenomenal properties inside the head, but depends constitutively on causal, historical, or social-linguistic relations to the thinker's physical and communal environment. Rather than a single falsifiable sentence, it functions as a flourishing framework encompassing competing concrete theories—from uncompromising pure wide realism (`P-116`) to mixed internal-external accounts (`P-117`) and contextualist variants (`P-118`)—that philosophers evaluate by how well they accommodate self-knowledge, memory, rational inference, and epistemic justification.

## Core Apparatus

### Core Theories and Propositions
- [[Content Externalism Holds]] (`P-40`) — Baseline existential thesis that some mental states have environmentally individuated contents.
- [[Two-Concept Content Externalism]] (`P-41`) — Composite theory pairing `P-40` with two-concept cohabitation and originary memory fixation (`D-21`) across slow switches.
- [[Pure Wide Realist Content Externalism]] (`P-116`) — Pure anti-individualism holding that wide environmental content does all genuine intentional explanatory work without a narrow content layer.
- [[Mixed Internal-External Content Externalism]] (`P-117`) — Non-Millian hybrid externalism pairing wide environmental reference with internal cognitive differentiators.
- [[Contextualist Content Externalism]] (`P-118`) — Externalism coupled with context-sensitive content attribution across switch episodes (bridging into [[Metasemantic Contextualism]], `S-03`).

### Core Concepts
- [[Content-Preserving Memory and Slow-Switching Dynamics]] (`C-05`) — Models how standing memories behave after undetected environmental relocation (originary fixation, anaphoric preservation, displacement, or fusion).
- [[Knowledge of Content]] (`C-06`) — Separates privileged single-token self-knowledge from comparative discriminatory knowledge of sameness and difference of content.

## Development Across Literature

Originating in Hilary Putnam's Twin Earth natural-kind cases and [[Tyler Burge]]'s social anti-individualism (arthritis / incomplete understanding and content-inheritance self-knowledge), the program came under sustained epistemological and logical pressure from [[Paul Boghossian]]'s slow-switching and transparency arguments ([[Boghossian 1992 - Externalism and Inference]], [[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]], [[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]]). Targeting [[Pure Wide Realist Content Externalism]] (`P-116`), Boghossian argued that undetected switching between Earth and Twin Earth introduces undetectable equivocation (`ARG-01`), defeating comparative transparency of difference (`ARG-02`), token-wide a priori logical abilities (`ARG-03`), and pure wide rationalizing explanation (`ARG-05`), while co-reference cases like Pierre and Paderewski defeat transparency of sameness (`ARG-04`).

Subsequent externalist work split into distinct defensive and revisionary trajectories:
- **Univocality and memory-preservation defenses:** [[Stephen Schiffer]] appealed to univocal referential intent to block slow-switch equivocation (`ARG-06`; [[Schiffer 1992 - Boghossian on Externalism and Inference]], answered in [[Boghossian 1992 - Reply to Schiffer]]), while [[Tyler Burge]]'s anaphoric memory preservation (`D-22`) inspired [[Sanford C. Goldberg]]'s examination of discursive justification ([[Goldberg-2007-Anti-Individualism, Content Preservation, and Discursive Justification]]).
- **Revisionary pure wide anti-individualism:** [[Jessica Brown]] ([[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]]), [[John M. Collins]] ([[Collins 2008 - Content Externalism and Brute Logical Error]]), [[Sanford C. Goldberg]] ([[Goldberg 1999 - The Relevance of Discriminatory Knowledge of Content]], [[Goldberg-2008-Must Differences in Cognitive Value be Transparent]]), and [[Mikkel Gerken]] ([[Gerken 2011 - Conceptual Equivocation and Warrant by Reasoning]]) largely accept [[Pure Wide Realist Content Externalism]] (`P-116`) and [[Two-Concept Content Externalism]] (`P-41`), biting the bullet on comparative transparency failures (`P-10`, `P-11`) and developing non-Cartesian accounts of blameless rationality (`ARG-19`), default entitlement, public cognitive value, and dual-component warrant.
- **Mixed, contextualist, and global extensions:** [[Manuel Pérez Otero]] deployed [[Mixed Internal-External Content Externalism]] (`P-117`) to reverse Boghossian's inference argument (`ARG-07`; [[Otero 2012 - Boghossian's Inference Argument against Content Externalism Reversed]], answered in [[Boghossian 2014 - Reply to Otero's Boghossian's Inference Argument against Content Externalism Reversed]]); [[Robert Stalnaker]] and [[Henry Jackman]] developed [[Contextualist Content Externalism]] (`P-118`; [[Jackman 2015 - Externalism, metasemantic contextualism, and self-knowledge]]); [[Mahmoud Morvarid]] pressed a conditional incompatibility between `P-41` and accessibilist [[Justification Internalism]] (`S-02`; [[Morvarid 2021 - A new argument for the incompatibility of content externalism with justification internalism]]); and [[Roberto Horácio de Sá Pereira]] pushed anti-individualism to *global content externalism*, rejecting any principled boundary between externally and internally individuated concepts ([[de Sá Pereira 2019 - Dangerous Liaisons]]).
