# Justification Internalism (`S-02`)

## Guiding Idea

`Justification Internalism` is the epistemological research program holding that whether a subject is epistemically justified in holding a belief depends exclusively on factors internal to the subject's own mental life or reflectively accessible perspective, rather than on external reliability or environmental facts outside the subject's ken. Because internalists disagree sharply over whether internality requires *reflective accessibility* to justifying grounds or only *supervenience on internal mental states*, the program bifurcates into accessibilist (`P-119`) and mentalist (`P-120`) sub-programs with very different vulnerability profiles under [[Content Externalism]] (`S-01`).

## Core Apparatus

### Core Theories and Propositions
- [[Accessibilist Justification Internalism]] (`P-119`) — Requires that every epistemically justified belief have at least one sufficient justification-conferring factor (`J-factor`) accessible to the subject by reflection alone (encompassing `JFA` and access-internalist supervenience).
- [[Mentalist Justification Internalism]] (`P-120`) — Requires that epistemic justification supervene strictly on a subject's internal non-relational mental states without demanding higher-order reflective access to those grounds.

### Core Concepts
- [[Transparency of Mental Content]] (`C-01`) — Governs whether subjects can reflectively recognize sameness or difference of content across inferential steps.
- [[Knowledge of Content]] (`C-06`) — Separates first-order privileged self-knowledge from comparative discriminatory knowledge needed to verify inferential links.
- [[Inferential Warrant]] (`C-11`) — Tracks how justificatory status is transmitted across multi-step deductive and memory-involving reasoning.

### Key Single-Source Arguments
- [[Morvarid 2021 - A new argument for the incompatibility of content externalism with justification internalism#Arguments]] — Refutes traditional privileged-access incompatibility routes via the content-neutral demonstrative token `J-factor` (`H`), then derives a conditional incompatibility between [[Two-Concept Content Externalism]] (`P-41`) and [[Accessibilist Justification Internalism]] (`P-119`) via reliability-based defeat of [[Transparency of Sameness of Content]] (`P-10`).
- [[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism#Arguments]] — Uses Shoemaker's Rita case to refute higher-order reflective explicatory access (`P-15`) and defends narrow Fregean cognitive value via Differential Dubitability, separating mentalist internalism from accessibilism.
- [[Goldberg-2007-Anti-Individualism, Content Preservation, and Discursive Justification#Arguments]] — Establishes a dilemma (`I1` vs. `I2`) showing that anti-individualism undermines reflectively computable discursive justification in extended multi-step reasoning.

## Development Across Literature

In contemporary epistemology, Earl Conee and Richard Feldman's separation of *mentalism* (`P-120`) from *accessibilism* (`P-119`) reframed longstanding disputes over whether [[Content Externalism]] (`S-01`) threatens internalist epistemology. Earlier incompatibilists (including BonJour, Vahid, and Brown) argued that if externalism undermines privileged access to thought content via slow switching or McKinsey reasoning, then justified wide-content beliefs lack reflectively accessible grounds.

Three lines of work in this corpus sharpen that dialectic:
- **Discursive justification under anti-individualism:** [[Sanford C. Goldberg]] ([[Goldberg-2007-Anti-Individualism, Content Preservation, and Discursive Justification]]) showed that even if anaphoric memory preserves univocality and deductive validity, anti-individualism still breaks access-internalist ideals of *reflectively computable discursive justification* across memory-dependent inferences.
- **Separating mentalism from accessibilism:** [[Brie Gertler]] ([[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism]]) explicitly rejected accessibilism (`P-119`) and reflective explicatory access (`P-15`) as red herrings, adapting the mentalist model (`P-120`) to philosophy of mind to argue that cognitive value supervenes narrowly on first-order internal rational dispositions without requiring higher-order reflective access.
- **From privileged access to transparency of sameness:** [[Mahmoud Morvarid]] ([[Morvarid 2021 - A new argument for the incompatibility of content externalism with justification internalism]]) showed that standard privileged-access arguments against `P-119` fail because a slow-switched thinker (Oscar) can appeal to a content-neutral demonstrative token `J-factor` (`H`), and reconstructed a stronger conditional conflict targeting `P-119` (`JFA`): under two-concept externalism (`P-41`), Oscar's unreliable sameness judgments defeat [[Transparency of Sameness of Content]] (`P-10`) and block reflective access to every available `J-factor` for his justified disjunctive belief.
