# Comparative A Priori Transparency Definition (`D-01`)

## Formulation
Transparency of mental content denotes full comparative epistemic transparency: the a priori knowability of both sameness and difference of content across thought tokens.

## Explanation
This definition treats mental content as "transparent" only when a thinker can tell purely from armchair reflection—without empirical investigation—both when two thoughts share the same meaning (Transparency of Sameness of Content, or TSC) and when two thoughts differ in meaning (Transparency of Difference of Content, or TDC), a combined ability often called Authoritative Discriminative Self-Knowledge (DASK). It is the demanding notion behind Paul Boghossian's slow-switching objections to content externalism, where a thinker unknowingly transported from Earth to Twin Earth mixes a pre-switch memory about Earth water or Pavarotti with a post-switch perception of Twin-Earth "twater" or Twin Pavarotti without noticing the hidden shift in meaning. Under this reading, the broad claims [[Mental Content Is Epistemically Transparent]] (`P-02`) and [[Rational Inference Requires Transparency of Mental Content]] (`P-01`) sharpen into [[Full Comparative Epistemic Transparency Holds]] (`P-12`) and [[Rational Inference Requires Comparative A Priori Transparency]] (`P-13`).

**Defines:** [[Transparency of Mental Content]] (`C-01`)

## Usage in Literature

[[Paul Boghossian]] adopts this comparative reading as the standard notion of transparency in [[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]] and [[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]], arguing that rationalizing explanation requires it (`P-13`) and that externalist slow switching violates TDC. [[Jessica Brown]] ([[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]]) and [[John M. Collins]] ([[Collins 2008 - Content Externalism and Brute Logical Error]]) accept this sharpening of the transparency thesis in order to argue that anti-individualism falsifies it (`P-12`) while denying that rationality depends on it.
