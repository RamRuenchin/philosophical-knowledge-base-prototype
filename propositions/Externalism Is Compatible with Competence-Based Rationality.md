# Externalism Is Compatible with Competence-Based Rationality (`P-27`)

## Formulation

Content externalism is compatible with competence-based normal-environment inferential rationality and conditional epistemic warrant.

## Explanation

This thesis sharpens [[Content Externalism Is Compatible with Rational Inference]] (`P-04`) under [[Competence-Based Normal-Environment Rationality Definition]] (`D-15`). It says that deductive reasoning is rational and grants conditional epistemic warrant whenever it exercises a fallible reasoning competence that reliably produces valid inferences in *normal* environments—even if an abnormal episode like slow switching between Earth and Twin Earth secretly makes one particular inference equivocate. Mikkel Gerken champions this view to escape two traps at once: it saves content externalism from requiring every warranted inference to be valid, while avoiding a purely "blamelessness" standard that he refutes via blameless Pete (a reasoner who commits the gambler's fallacy blamelessly due to non-culpable cognitive limits and expert-backed community norms, yet still lacks genuine inferential warrant).

## Usage in Literature

In [[Gerken 2011 - Conceptual Equivocation and Warrant by Reasoning]], [[Mikkel Gerken]] endorses this thesis to resolve his own Gerken Validity Argument Against Anti-Individualism ([[Gerken 2011 - Conceptual Equivocation and Warrant by Reasoning#Arguments]]). Rather than relying on transparency or mere deontological blamelessness (which he refutes via Pete's gambler's fallacy in Gambler Fallacy Refutation of Epistemic Deontologism ([[Gerken 2011 - Conceptual Equivocation and Warrant by Reasoning#Arguments]])), Gerken rejects the unrestricted token-validity requirement in favor of competence-based conditional warrant, keeping anti-individualism compatible with warranted deductive reasoning.
