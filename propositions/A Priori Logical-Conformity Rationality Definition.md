# A Priori Logical-Conformity Rationality Definition (`D-12`)

## Formulation
Rationality and inferential normativity denotes a priori logical-conformity rationality, on which rational inference and rationalizing explanation require a priori access to wide logical relations.

## Explanation
Named "B-rationality" (after Paul Boghossian) by Jessica Brown, this demanding standard holds that being a rational thinker—and having your inferences explained as rational—requires being able to conform your environmentally determined ("wide") thoughts to logic purely from armchair reflection and avoid undetectable equivocation. Because an unknowingly slow-switched thinker mixing an Earth memory with a Twin-Earth perception cannot detect that shift from the armchair, this standard generates the sharpest apparent conflict between content externalism and rationality, contrasting with softer standards like [[Blameless Identity-Sensitivity Rationality Definition]] (`D-14`) and [[Competence-Based Normal-Environment Rationality Definition]] (`D-15`). Under this reading, [[Content Externalism Is Compatible with Rational Inference]] (`P-04`) sharpens into [[Externalism Is Compatible with A Priori Conformity Rationality]] (`P-24`).

**Defines:** [[Rationality and Inferential Normativity]] (`C-03`)

## Usage in Literature

[[Paul Boghossian]] relies on this stringent model of rationality in [[Boghossian 1992 - Externalism and Inference]] and [[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]] to argue that externalist slow switching undermines rationalizing explanation; [[Tyler Burge]] likewise treats rational coherence as demanding preserved logical relations (via anaphoric memory, `D-22`) rather than surrendering rationality to equivocation. [[Jessica Brown]] explicitly isolates and critiques B-rationality in [[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]], arguing that human beings are not B-rational independently of externalism and proposing blameless sensitivity (`D-14`) instead.
