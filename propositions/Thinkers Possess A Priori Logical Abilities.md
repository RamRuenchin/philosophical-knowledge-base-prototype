# Thinkers Possess A Priori Logical Abilities (`P-03`)

## Formulation

Thinkers possess a priori logical abilities to evaluate the validity and consistency of reasoning.

## Explanation

This broad claim says that thinkers possess *a priori logical abilities* (often abbreviated `APLA`)—the capacity to tell through reasoning alone, without empirical fact-checking, whether an inference is logically valid or self-contradictory. That ability looks very different depending on whether it applies to concrete thoughts whose meanings depend on the outside world, to abstract logical patterns like *modus ponens*, or only to internal mental symbols. Separating these three sharpenings of [[A Priori Logical Abilities]] (`C-02`) clarifies why slow-switching thought experiments—where a thinker like Peter unknowingly moves from Earth to Twin Earth and mixes an Earth memory with a Twin Earth perception—threaten concrete wide-content logic without undermining abstract logical competence.

### Disambiguation Sharpenings
- Under `D-09` ([[Token-Level Wide APLA Definition]]) -> [[Token-Level Wide APLA Holds]] (`P-21`): thinkers can tell purely from reflection whether a specific real-world inference, whose meanings depend on the external environment, is logically valid or secretly equivocates.
- Under `D-10` ([[Form-Level APLA Definition]]) -> [[Form-Level APLA Holds]] (`P-22`): thinkers can tell purely from reflection which abstract logical patterns (such as "if A then B; A; therefore B") are valid, regardless of empirical facts about what specific words refer to.
- Under `D-11` ([[Narrow-Syntactic APLA Definition]]) -> [[Narrow-Syntactic APLA Holds]] (`P-23`): thinkers have an inward sensitivity to the internal syntactic form of their thoughts, explaining why an inference feels valid from inside even if its external referents silently shift.

## Usage in Literature

In [[Boghossian 1992 - Externalism and Inference]] and [[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]], [[Paul Boghossian]] uses slow-switched Peter's undetectable equivocation to argue that externalism defeats token-level wide APLA (`P-21`; [[Slow-Switch Equivocation vs. Token-Wide APLA]], `ARG-03`), while later defending narrow-syntactic APLA (`P-23`) and narrow syntactic preparedness (`P-61`) in [[Boghossian 2014 - Reply to Otero's Boghossian's Inference Argument against Content Externalism Reversed]]. [[Manuel Pérez Otero]] endorses `P-03` under the form-level sharpening (`P-22`) in [[Otero 2012 - Boghossian's Inference Argument against Content Externalism Reversed]], arguing that externalism preserves abstract logical competence while Peter's puzzle is theory-neutral across content theories.
