# Token-Level Wide APLA Definition (`D-09`)

## Formulation
A priori logical abilities denotes the a priori capacity to assess the validity or invalidity of concrete thought-token inferences whose contents are environmentally individuated.

## Explanation
A priori logical abilities (APLA) are here defined in a strong, concrete sense: the ability to tell purely from armchair reflection whether a specific, real-life step of reasoning involving environmentally determined ("wide") contents is logically valid or secretly equivocates. Unlike merely grasping general logical rules ([[Form-Level APLA Definition]], `D-10`), this definition demands that a thinker be able to spot invalidity in particular dated thoughts—which is directly threatened when slow-switched Peter unknowingly moves from Earth to Twin Earth and mixes an Earth memory of Pavarotti or water with a Twin-Earth perception without realizing the concepts differ. Under this reading, [[Thinkers Possess A Priori Logical Abilities]] (`P-03`) sharpens into [[Token-Level Wide APLA Holds]] (`P-21`).

**Defines:** [[A Priori Logical Abilities]] (`C-02`)

## Usage in Literature

[[Paul Boghossian]] relies on this token-level wide sharpening in [[Boghossian 1992 - Externalism and Inference]] and [[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]], arguing that slow-switched Peter's undetectable equivocation contradicts `P-21`. [[Manuel Pérez Otero]] critiques this reading in [[Otero 2012 - Boghossian's Inference Argument against Content Externalism Reversed]], arguing that thinkers possess only form-level APLA (`D-10`).
