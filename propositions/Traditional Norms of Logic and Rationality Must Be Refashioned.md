# Traditional Norms of Logic and Rationality Must Be Refashioned (`P-94`)

## Formulation

Traditional idealized norms equating rationality with infallible a priori logical conformity must be refashioned.

## Explanation

This thesis calls for revising classical, overly idealized standards of rationality—such as [[Paul Boghossian]]'s "B-rationality," which defines being rational in terms of an infallible, purely armchair ability to spot logical connections and keep one's thoughts logically consistent. Refashioning these norms means replacing strict a priori logical conformity with realistic standards that separate genuinely culpable irrationality from blameless, introspectively undetectable mistakes ([[Brute Logical Error Is Possible]], `P-93`).

## Usage in Literature

[[Jessica Brown]] endorses `P-94` in [[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]], deriving it via [[Argument from Logical Norms Dilemma (Brown and Collins)]] (`ARG-19`) and replacing B-rationality—which empirical psychology and synonymy puzzles already undermine—with a blameless-error criterion ([[Externalism Is Compatible with Blameless Identity-Sensitivity Rationality]], `P-26`; `D-14`). [[John M. Collins]] treats `P-94` as plausible in [[Collins 2008 - Content Externalism and Brute Logical Error]], framing it as the central upshot of the Argument from Logical Norms once blocking strategies fail, while leaving open whether externalists should adopt Brown's blaming policy or appeal to default defeasible entitlement in stable environments.
