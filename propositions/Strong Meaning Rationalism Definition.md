# Strong Meaning Rationalism Definition (`D-05`)

## Formulation
Transparency of mental content denotes an infallible a priori grasp of the identity, difference, and non-emptiness of all of one's concepts purely from within.

## Explanation
Strong Meaning Rationalism (SMR) is the maximally demanding claim that a thinker can tell infallibly from armchair reflection alone whether any two of their concepts are the same or different, and even whether each concept successfully refers to something real rather than being empty. Ruth Garrett Millikan uses this extreme target to argue that content externalism refutes transparency, whereas Tadeusz Szubka argues that defeating this overly ambitious ideal leaves modest, fallible synchronic transparency ([[Modest Synchronic Transparency Definition]], `D-04`) intact. Under this reading, [[Mental Content Is Epistemically Transparent]] (`P-02`) sharpens into [[Strong Meaning Rationalism Holds]] (`P-20`).

**Defines:** [[Transparency of Mental Content]] (`C-01`)

## Usage in Literature

[[Ruth Garrett Millikan]] frames transparency via this Strong Meaning Rationalism target in order to refute it from an externalist, third-person perspective (reconstructed in [[Szubka 2000 - Meaning Rationalism, A Priori, and Transparency of Content]]). [[Tadeusz Szubka]] agrees in [[Szubka 2000 - Meaning Rationalism, A Priori, and Transparency of Content]] that externalism defeats `P-20` under `D-05`, but argues that Millikan conflates this overly strong target with modest synchronic transparency (`D-04`).
