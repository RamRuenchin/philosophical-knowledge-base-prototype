# Public Linguistic Standards Partly Determine Cognitive Value (`P-79`)

## Formulation

Public linguistic standards partly determine the cognitive value of a thinker's mental contents.

## Explanation

A thought's [[Cognitive Value|cognitive value]] is the aspect of its content that matters for rational judgment, belief revision, and inference (for example, why believing "Hesperus is bright" differs cognitively from believing "Phosphorus is bright"). This thesis claims that cognitive value is not determined solely inside an individual's head, but is partly fixed by shared public linguistic standards and communal norms. Its main motivation is testimony: speakers and hearers routinely transmit specific beliefs and knowledge through words despite knowing little of one another's idiosyncratic interpretive habits, which works because individuals defer to public language standards.

## Usage in Literature

[[Sanford C. Goldberg]] defends `P-79` in [[Goldberg-2008-Must Differences in Cognitive Value be Transparent]], arguing that routine testimonial transmission of specific beliefs and knowledge—between speakers and hearers who know little of one another's idiosyncratic interpretive dispositions—would collapse into an unstable coordination problem unless shared public linguistic standards and semantic deference partly constituted an individual's epistemic perspective (`D-35`, [[Goldberg-2008-Must Differences in Cognitive Value be Transparent#Arguments]]). [[Tyler Burge]] likewise endorses `P-79` (reconstructed across [[Goldberg-2008-Must Differences in Cognitive Value be Transparent]] and [[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism]]), proposing that rational differential doubt is governed by publicly recognized methods and communal norms. [[Brie Gertler]] rejects `P-79` in [[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism]], arguing via Burge's arthritis case that [[Cognitive Value]] supervenes strictly on internal first-order rational dispositions (including internal deferential dispositions) without being constitutively fixed by external public standards ([[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism#Arguments]]).
