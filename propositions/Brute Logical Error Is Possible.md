# Brute Logical Error Is Possible (`P-93`)

## Formulation

Possibly, a fully rational and cognitively blameless thinker commits an introspectively undetectable logical error.

## Explanation

A "brute logical error" (adapting [[Tyler Burge]]'s idea of brute error) is a genuine logical mistake—such as unknowingly shifting between two different meanings of a word in an argument (equivocation) or holding an unrecognized contradiction—that occurs even though the thinker is completely rational, careful, and cognitively well-functioning. This thesis asserts that such errors are genuinely possible whenever external environmental factors determine thought contents in ways that the thinker cannot detect from the inside by reflection alone.

- Defeasibly supported by empirical Wason-task fallacies, Mates puzzles, and ordinary referential-confusion cases (e.g., Faria's Golden Retrievers) showing that cognitively well-functioning thinkers can commit undetectable logical errors ([[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]], [[Faria 2009 - Unsafe reasoning A survey]]).

## Usage in Literature

`P-93` commands broad externalist endorsement across the corpus. [[Jessica Brown]] endorses it in [[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]] and deploys it in [[Argument from Logical Norms Dilemma (Brown and Collins)]] (`ARG-19`) to argue that traditional norms of rationality must be refashioned (`P-94`). [[John M. Collins]] endorses `P-93` in [[Collins 2008 - Content Externalism and Brute Logical Error]] after refuting three compatibilist blocking strategies (denying twin concepts, anaphoric content links, and enthymematic identity premises). [[Mikkel Gerken]] endorses `P-93` in [[Gerken 2011 - Conceptual Equivocation and Warrant by Reasoning]] as an instance of well-functioning fallibility in slow-switched reasoning, and [[Paulo Faria]] endorses `P-93` in [[Faria 2009 - Unsafe reasoning A survey]] within his analysis of unsafe reasoning and logical luck.
