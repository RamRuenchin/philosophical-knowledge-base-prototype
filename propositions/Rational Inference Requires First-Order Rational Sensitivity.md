# Rational Inference Requires First-Order Rational Sensitivity (`P-14`)

## Formulation

Rational inference requires first-order rational sensitivity to content relations in belief updating and reasoning without requiring higher-order reflective access.

## Explanation

Sharpening [[Rational Inference Requires Transparency of Mental Content]] (`P-01`) under [[First-Order Rational Sensitivity Definition]] (`D-06`), this modest thesis says that reasoning rationally only requires responding sensibly to how your beliefs fit together at the ground level—for instance, revising a plan or drawing a conclusion appropriately when you learn something new. You do *not* need the higher-order ability to step back, analyze your concepts, and verbally spell out the exact logical rules or truth conditions behind your move. Brie Gertler illustrates this with Shoemaker's Rita case, where Rita updates her beliefs and plans rationally in response to new evidence even though she cannot articulate her reasons or explain the exact definitions of her concepts.

## Inferential Relations
### Leads To
- Directly incompatible with [[Rational Inference Requires Reflective Explicatory Access]] (`P-15`) (implies its negation, since `P-14` denies that rational inference requires higher-order reflective access).

### Led To By / Challenged By

## Usage in Literature

Sharpening [[Rational Inference Requires Transparency of Mental Content]] (`P-01`) under [[First-Order Rational Sensitivity Definition]] (`D-06`), `P-14` is endorsed by [[Brie Gertler]] in [[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism]]. Drawing on Shoemaker's Rita case, Gertler argues that rational updating and inference depend on cognitive values systematically governing first-order transitions rather than on higher-order reflective explicatory access ([[Rational Inference Requires Reflective Explicatory Access]], `P-15`), noting that both internalists and externalists can accept `P-14` while contesting whether external factors directly shape those first-order rational relations.
