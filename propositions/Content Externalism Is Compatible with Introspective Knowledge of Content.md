# Content Externalism Is Compatible with Introspective Knowledge of Content (`P-05`)

## Formulation

Content externalism is compatible with authoritative introspective knowledge of mental content.

## Explanation

This broad thesis asks whether [[Content Externalism]] (`S-01`)—the view that your physical environment and linguistic community partly determine what your thoughts mean—allows you to know what you are thinking simply by looking inward, without empirical investigation. Debates over [[Knowledge of Content]] (`C-06`) turn on a crucial split: knowing the content of a single thought you are currently having (Knowledge of Content Proper, `KC`) is widely considered compatible with externalism, whereas being able to tell from introspection alone whether two thoughts mean the same thing or differ from Twin Earth counterparts (Discriminatory Knowledge of Content, `DKC`) is widely seen as incompatible.

### Disambiguation Sharpenings
- Under `D-25` ([[Knowledge of Content Proper Definition]]) -> [[Externalism Is Compatible with Knowledge of Content Proper]] (`P-30`): externalism is compatible with authoritative first-person knowledge of what a single current thought is about (Knowledge of Content Proper, or `KC`).
- Under `D-26` ([[Discriminatory Knowledge of Content Definition]]) -> [[Externalism Is Compatible with Discriminatory Knowledge of Content]] (`P-31`): externalism is compatible with the introspective ability to tell whether two thoughts share or differ in meaning without checking the external world (Discriminatory Knowledge of Content, or `DKC`).

## Usage in Literature

[[Tyler Burge]] endorses `P-05` under the KC sharpening (`P-30`) via self-verifying present-tense self-ascriptions, a compatibility also defended via relevant alternatives in [[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]] and via content inheritance in [[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism]]. [[Sanford C. Goldberg]] rejects `P-05` under the DKC sharpening (`P-31`) in [[Goldberg 1999 - The Relevance of Discriminatory Knowledge of Content]], bracketing KC compatibilism while demonstrating via Sally's "pragmatist" case that failures of DKC still directly constrain truth-directed reasoning even when KC is granted.
