# Slow-Switched Peter Is Intuitively Rational (`P-48`)

## Formulation

If both (both (both some mental states have contents that constitutively depend on external environmental factors and slow switching produces two-concept cohabitation with originary memory fixation) and (possibly, a slow-switched thinker performs a subjectively valid syllogism combining a pre-switch memory premise with a post-switch experiential premise)) and (if a slow-switched thinker performs a subjectively valid syllogism combining a pre-switch memory premise with a post-switch experiential premise, then that thinker commits an introspectively undetectable equivocation between distinct wide contents), then some purportedly deductive inference that generates conditional epistemic warrant commits an equivocation between distinct wide contents.

## Explanation

In Boghossian's slow-switching scenario (`P-46`), Peter unknowingly moves from Earth to Twin Earth and reasons from a pre-switch Earth memory (about Earth Pavarotti or water) together with a post-switch Twin-Earth perception (about Twin Pavarotti or "twater"). Intuitively, Peter commits no defect of reasoning—his inference makes full sense from his internal point of view and gives him *conditional epistemic warrant* (justificatory support for the conclusion, conditional on his premises being warranted), just as an unswitched duplicate's inference would. Combined with two-concept [[Content Externalism]] (the view that mental contents depend on one's environment and that slow switching leaves a thinker holding old Earth concepts in memory alongside new Twin-Earth concepts) and the verdict that Peter unwittingly equivocates (`P-47`), this entails that a purportedly deductive inference generating conditional epistemic warrant can still equivocate between two distinct environmentally determined ("wide") contents.

## Usage in Literature

This intuitive verdict is widely endorsed across opposing camps: [[Paul Boghossian]] ([[Boghossian 1992 - Externalism and Inference]], [[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]]) uses Peter's intuitive rationality to argue that pure wide externalism collapses the distinction between factual ignorance and logical irrationality; [[Stephen Schiffer]] ([[Schiffer 1992 - Boghossian on Externalism and Inference]]) and [[Robert Stalnaker]] (as reconstructed in [[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]]) accept the intuition while explaining it via univocal intent or contextual presupposition; and [[Mikkel Gerken]] ([[Gerken 2011 - Conceptual Equivocation and Warrant by Reasoning]]) builds it directly into Gerken Validity Argument Against Anti-Individualism ([[Gerken 2011 - Conceptual Equivocation and Warrant by Reasoning#Arguments]]), upholding `P-48` and anti-individualism at the expense of the unrestricted validity requirement.
