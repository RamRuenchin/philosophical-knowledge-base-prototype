# Unstructured Possible-Worlds Propositions Definition (`D-43`)

## Formulation
Mental representation vehicle denotes unstructured sets of possible worlds serving as complete semantic values of attitudes without sub-propositional concepts, Fregean senses, or mental files.

## Explanation
This anti-representationalist view represents the content of a belief simply as an unstructured set of possible worlds—the set of ways the world could be if the belief were true—doing away with building-block concepts, Fregean senses, or mental files as needless "creatures of darkness." Paired with Robert Stalnaker's [[Radical Interpreter-Relative Contextualism Holds]] (`P-33`) and his rejection of [[The Common View of Mental Content]] (`P-44`), it contrasts directly with [[Minimalist Person-Level Mental Files Definition]] (`D-42`), which Laura Schroeter argues is still needed to capture stable internal relations of apparent co-reference.

**Defines:** [[Mental Representation Vehicle]] (`C-13`)

## Usage in Literature

[[Robert Stalnaker]] (as reconstructed in [[Schroeter 2013 - Are Concepts Creatures of Darkness]] and [[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]]) endorses `D-43` and its substantive thesis [[Unstructured Possible-Worlds Propositions Suffice Without Concepts]] (`P-90`). [[Laura Schroeter]] argues in [[Schroeter 2013 - Are Concepts Creatures of Darkness]] that while unstructured possible-worlds sets suffice for simple stimulus–response systems, they cannot directly represent stable internal mind–mind relations of apparent *de jure* co-reference, forcing Stalnaker into fragmented contextual "spot-checks."
