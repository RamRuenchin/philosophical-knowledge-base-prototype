# Rational Inference Requires Transparency of Mental Content (`P-01`)

## Formulation

Rational inference requires epistemic transparency of mental content.

## Explanation

In everyday terms, this broad thesis claims that for your reasoning to count as rational, you must have reliable inward access to what your own thoughts mean. Because knowing what your thoughts mean can be understood in four very different ways—from simply responding sensibly to your beliefs on the fly, to explicitly explaining your reasons, to telling from reflection alone whether any two thoughts share the same meaning—philosophers often talk past one another when they argue over [[Transparency of Mental Content]] (`C-01`). Disambiguating these four sharpenings reveals that almost everyone accepts basic first-order responsiveness while clashing sharply over stronger a priori access.

### Disambiguation Sharpenings
- Under `D-01` ([[Comparative A Priori Transparency Definition]]) -> [[Rational Inference Requires Comparative A Priori Transparency]] (`P-13`): rational reasoning requires being able to tell from reflection alone, without empirical investigation, whether any two of your thoughts mean the same thing or different things.
- Under `D-06` ([[First-Order Rational Sensitivity Definition]]) -> [[Rational Inference Requires First-Order Rational Sensitivity]] (`P-14`): rational reasoning requires only that you update and connect your beliefs sensibly at the ground level, without needing to step back and articulate your reasons.
- Under `D-07` ([[Reflective Explicatory Access Definition]]) -> [[Rational Inference Requires Reflective Explicatory Access]] (`P-15`): rational reasoning requires the higher-order reflective ability to spell out your reasons and explain what makes your thoughts true.
- Under `D-08` ([[Reasoning-Transparency Definition]]) -> [[Rational Inference Requires Reasoning-Transparency]] (`P-16`): rational reasoning must be determined purely by internal logical form, so you can tell from inside which inference you are making regardless of your physical environment or language community.

## Usage in Literature

Authors divide sharply on `P-01` largely because they target different sharpenings. [[Paul Boghossian]] endorses `P-01` under `D-01` (`P-13`) in [[Boghossian 1992 - Externalism and Inference]] and [[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]], using it to argue that externalist transparency failures collapse pure wide rationalizing explanation. [[Brie Gertler]] endorses `P-01` only under `D-06` (`P-14`) in [[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism]], while rejecting the reflective explicatory sharpening (`P-15`) via Shoemaker's Rita case (Rita Case Refutation of Reflective Explicatory Access ([[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism#Arguments]])). Conversely, [[Jessica Brown]] rejects `P-01` under `D-01` (`P-13`) in [[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]] by denying that rationality requires a priori comparative access, and [[Roberto Horácio de Sá Pereira]] rejects `P-01` under `D-08` (`P-16`) in [[de Sá Pereira 2019 - Dangerous Liaisons]] because global externalism materially individuates reasoning (Reasoning Is Both Formally and Materially Individuated ([[de Sá Pereira 2019 - Dangerous Liaisons#Propositions]])).
