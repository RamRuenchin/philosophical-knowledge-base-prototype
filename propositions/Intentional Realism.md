# Intentional Realism (`P-42`)

## Formulation

Intentional states have determinate, interpreter-independent representational contents.

## Explanation

In plain terms, *Intentional Realism* holds that your beliefs, desires, and thoughts genuinely represent specific states of affairs with definite truth conditions—there is an objective fact of the matter about what you think that does not change depending on who is interpreting you or what conversational goals an observer happens to have. Paul Boghossian pairs it with Fregeanism (`P-43`) as the first half of [[The Common View of Mental Content]] (`P-44`) and defends it against Robert Stalnaker's radical interpreter-relative contextualism (`P-33`), arguing that making one person's thought contents depend on an interpreter's beliefs and goals starts an infinite regress unless at least some mental states have determinate, interpreter-independent contents.

## Usage in Literature

In [[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]], [[Paul Boghossian]] introduces Intentional Realism as the first conjunct of [[The Common View of Mental Content]] (`P-44`) and endorses it against [[Robert Stalnaker]]'s radical contextualism (`P-33`). Reconstructed via [[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]], [[Schroeter 2013 - Are Concepts Creatures of Darkness]], and [[Jackman 2015 - Externalism, metasemantic contextualism, and self-knowledge]], Stalnaker rejects Intentional Realism as a Cartesian residue, holding that content attributions depend on an attributor's explanatory purposes. Boghossian counters that making subject A's content depend on attributor B's beliefs and purposes already presupposes determinate facts about B's intentional states, threatening regress unless Intentional Realism holds for at least some mental contents.
