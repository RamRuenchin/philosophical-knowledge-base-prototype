# Co-Referential Guise Opacity Verdict (`P-51`)

## Formulation

If a rational thinker holds two distinct co-referential mental guises without realizing that they co-refer, then that thinker cannot know a priori that the corresponding thought tokens share wide content.

## Explanation

In unwitting co-reference cases (`P-50`)—such as Kripke's Pierre (who does not realize "Londres" and "London" name the same city) or the Paderewski puzzle (where a thinker does not realize Paderewski the pianist and Paderewski the politician are one person)—the fact that both mental guises pick out the same object is an empirical fact about the external world. This verdict states that no amount of purely introspective reflection enables the thinker to discover *a priori* (independently of empirical investigation) that the corresponding thought tokens share the same environmentally determined ("wide") content.

## Usage in Literature

Both [[Paul Boghossian]] (in [[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]]) and [[Jessica Brown]] (in [[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]], using the Rudolf/Paderewski variant) endorse this opacity verdict and combine it with `P-40`, `P-50`, and bridge `P-66` in [[Pierre and Paderewski Opacity vs. Transparency of Sameness]] (`ARG-04`) to refute Transparency of Sameness of Content (`P-10`) under non-Fregean wide content externalism. Boghossian uses this failure alongside slow-switching failures of Transparency of Difference to press the transparency challenge against pure wide externalism, whereas Brown accepts the failure of TSC to argue against the idealized transparency requirement (`P-13`).
