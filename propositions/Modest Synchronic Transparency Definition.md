# Modest Synchronic Transparency Definition (`D-04`)

## Formulation
Transparency of mental content denotes modest first-person synchronic conceptual transparency that is compatible with content externalism and fallible epistemic warrant.

## Explanation
This definition holds that at any given moment ("synchronically"), a thinker has a modest, fallible first-person grasp of how their concepts relate to one another, even if what those concepts refer to depends partly on the external environment. Proposed by Tadeusz Szubka as a middle ground, it rejects the extreme demand of Strong Meaning Rationalism ([[Strong Meaning Rationalism Definition]], `D-05`) that thinkers must have infallible, comprehensive armchair certainty about the identity, difference, and real-world reference of all their concepts. Under this reading, [[Mental Content Is Epistemically Transparent]] (`P-02`) sharpens into [[Modest Synchronic Transparency Holds]] (`P-19`).

**Defines:** [[Transparency of Mental Content]] (`C-01`)

## Usage in Literature

[[Tadeusz Szubka]] introduces and endorses this modest sharpening in [[Szubka 2000 - Meaning Rationalism, A Priori, and Transparency of Content]] to carve out middle ground between [[Ruth Garrett Millikan]]'s wholesale rejection of transparency and infallibilist rationalism (`D-05`).
