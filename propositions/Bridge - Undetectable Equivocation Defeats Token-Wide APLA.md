# Bridge - Undetectable Equivocation Defeats Token-Wide APLA (`P-114`)

## Formulation

If both (possibly, a slow-switched thinker performs a subjectively valid syllogism combining a pre-switch memory premise with a post-switch experiential premise) and (if a slow-switched thinker performs a subjectively valid syllogism combining a pre-switch memory premise with a post-switch experiential premise, then that thinker commits an introspectively undetectable equivocation between distinct wide contents), then it is not the case that every thinker can know a priori the logical validity or invalidity of every concrete wide-content inference that thinker performs.

## Explanation

This bridge links slow-switch equivocation to the failure of Token-Level Wide A Priori Logical Abilities (APLA)—the claim that a thinker can tell purely from armchair reflection whether any specific concrete inference she performs, individuated by its external ("wide") contents, is logically valid or invalid ([[Token-Level Wide APLA Holds]], `P-21`). In [[Paul Boghossian]]'s slow-switching thought experiment, Peter unknowingly moves from Earth to Twin Earth and combines a pre-switch memory premise (about Earth Pavarotti or Earth water) with a post-switch perceptual premise (about Twin Pavarotti or Twin-Earth twater) in a syllogism that appears completely valid from the inside yet shifts meanings mid-argument. If such an introspectively undetectable equivocation can occur, then thinkers plainly lack the a priori ability to verify the validity or invalidity of every concrete wide-content inference they perform.

## Usage in Literature

[[Paul Boghossian]] ([[Boghossian 1992 - Externalism and Inference]], [[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]]) uses `P-114` conditionally on two-concept externalism to argue that slow switching undermines concrete a priori logical abilities (`P-21`), while [[Manuel Pérez Otero]] ([[Otero 2012 - Boghossian's Inference Argument against Content Externalism Reversed]]) accepts the link against token-level wide APLA while preserving form-level APLA (`P-22`).
