# Peter Inference Remains Univocal via Anaphoric Memory (`P-49`)

## Formulation

If a slow-switched thinker performs a subjectively valid syllogism combining a pre-switch memory premise with a post-switch experiential premise, then anaphoric preservative memory keeps the premise tokens univocal without equivocation.

## Explanation

In Boghossian's slow-switching thought experiment (`P-46`), Peter unknowingly moves from Earth to Twin Earth and combines an Earth memory (about Earth Pavarotti or water) with a Twin-Earth perception (about Twin Pavarotti or "twater") in what feels like a valid syllogism. Rather than treating Peter's terms as secretly shifting meaning, this verdict applies an *anaphoric* (back-referencing) model of [[Content-Preserving Memory and Slow-Switching Dynamics|preservative memory]] (`D-22`): because Peter intends to reason about one single person or substance throughout his argument, later concept tokens inherit the reference of earlier ones. His premises therefore stay univocal (carrying one uniform meaning), making his syllogism deductively valid—with one false premise—rather than an equivocation.

**Direct incompatibility:** Directly incompatible with [[Peter Commits Undetectable Equivocation]] (`P-47`).

## Usage in Literature

[[Stephen Schiffer]] (in [[Schiffer 1992 - Boghossian on Externalism and Inference]], arguing via the Yolanda twin case and single-premise conjoined restatement) and [[Tyler Burge]] (as reconstructed across [[Goldberg-2007-Anti-Individualism, Content Preservation, and Discursive Justification]] and [[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]]) endorse this univocality verdict to block slow-switch equivocation. [[Manuel Pérez Otero]] (in [[Otero 2012 - Boghossian's Inference Argument against Content Externalism Reversed]]) treats the anaphoric solution as one of the two most plausible externalist replies. [[Paul Boghossian]] rejects it in [[Boghossian 1992 - Reply to Schiffer]] (arguing that externalist causal fixation still permits combining independently grounded Earth and Twin-Earth memories) and later cites premise-order sensitivity against it, while [[Sanford C. Goldberg]] (in [[Goldberg-2007-Anti-Individualism, Content Preservation, and Discursive Justification]]) situates it as the second horn of his content-preservation dilemma, arguing that forcing univocality shifts remembered content and undermines memorial justification preservation ([[Goldberg-2007-Anti-Individualism, Content Preservation, and Discursive Justification#Arguments]]). It is further challenged by [[John M. Collins]]'s critique of anaphoric links in [[Collins 2008 - Content Externalism and Brute Logical Error]] and by [[Henry Jackman]] as overly sensitive to premise order in [[Jackman 2015 - Externalism, metasemantic contextualism, and self-knowledge]].
