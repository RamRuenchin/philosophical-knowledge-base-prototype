# Boghossian Peter Slow-Switching Setup Possibility (`P-46`)

## Formulation

Possibly, a slow-switched thinker performs a subjectively valid syllogism combining a pre-switch memory premise with a post-switch experiential premise.

## Explanation

In Paul Boghossian's classic "slow-switching" thought experiment, an ordinary person named Peter is unknowingly transported from Earth to Twin Earth—a planet identical to Earth except that its water-like liquid has a different chemical composition (called "twater") and its famous opera singer is a look-alike doppelgänger ("Twin Pavarotti"). After living on Twin Earth long enough to interact normally with its local environment, Peter performs a seemingly valid syllogism combining a pre-switch Earth memory (such as "Pavarotti once swam in Lake Taupo" or "There was water in the lake ten years ago") with a post-switch Twin-Earth perceptual belief (such as "The singer I heard yesterday is Pavarotti" or "There is water in the lake now"). This proposition states only that such a slow-switching scenario is genuinely possible, leaving open whether Peter's inference actually commits a hidden equivocation (`P-47`) or stays univocal—using one single meaning throughout (`P-49`).

Across its seven participating arguments, `P-46` serves as the shared neutral staging ground for deriving equivocation (`ARG-01`), challenging TDC (`ARG-02`), Token-Wide APLA (`ARG-03`), reflective discursive justification ([[Goldberg-2007-Anti-Individualism, Content Preservation, and Discursive Justification#Arguments]]), and the unrestricted validity requirement ([[Gerken 2011 - Conceptual Equivocation and Warrant by Reasoning#Arguments]]), as well as Jackman's contextualist refutation of equivocation ([[Jackman 2015 - Externalism, metasemantic contextualism, and self-knowledge#Arguments]]).
