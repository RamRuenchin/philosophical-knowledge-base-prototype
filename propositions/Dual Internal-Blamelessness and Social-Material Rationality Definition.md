# Dual Internal-Blamelessness and Social-Material Rationality Definition (`D-17`)

## Formulation
Rationality and inferential normativity denotes a dual standard distinguishing internal epistemic blamelessness from socially and materially individuated inferential validity.

## Explanation
This two-part definition separates two questions that are often conflated: first, whether a reasoner is internally blameless from their own perspective, and second, whether the inference itself is genuinely valid once social and environmental ("material") facts about meaning are taken into account. Roberto Horácio de Sá Pereira adopts this dual picture so that an unknowingly slow-switched thinker can count as internally blameless while still making an inference that fails socially and materially individuated validity. Under this reading, [[Content Externalism Is Compatible with Rational Inference]] (`P-04`) sharpens into [[Externalism Is Compatible with Dual Social-Material Rationality]] (`P-29`).

**Defines:** [[Rationality and Inferential Normativity]] (`C-03`)

## Usage in Literature

[[Roberto Horácio de Sá Pereira]] endorses this dual sharpening in [[de Sá Pereira 2019 - Dangerous Liaisons]] after arguing from global content externalism that reasoning is both formally and materially individuated, allowing slow-switched thinkers to remain internally blameless while failing material validity.
