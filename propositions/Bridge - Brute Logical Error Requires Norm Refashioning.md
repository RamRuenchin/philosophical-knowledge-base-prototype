# Bridge - Brute Logical Error Requires Norm Refashioning (`P-95`)

## Formulation

If (possibly, a fully rational and cognitively blameless thinker commits an introspectively undetectable logical error), then traditional idealized norms equating rationality with infallible a priori logical conformity must be refashioned.

## Explanation

This bridge connects the possibility of brute logical error ([[Brute Logical Error Is Possible]], `P-93`) directly to the need to revise traditional standards of rationality ([[Traditional Norms of Logic and Rationality Must Be Refashioned]], `P-94`). Traditional idealized norms treat it as a requirement of rationality that a thinker can avoid elementary logical mistakes purely from reflection; therefore, once we concede that a fully rational, cognitively blameless thinker can commit a logical error that is undetectable from the inside, those traditional norms cannot stand unchanged and must be rebuilt.

## Usage in Literature

[[Jessica Brown]] endorses `P-95` in [[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]] as the conditional bridge in [[Argument from Logical Norms Dilemma (Brown and Collins)]] (`ARG-19`), inferring [[Traditional Norms of Logic and Rationality Must Be Refashioned]] (`P-94`) from [[Brute Logical Error Is Possible]] (`P-93`). [[John M. Collins]] formalizes this exact conditional as Premise 3 of the Argument from Logical Norms in [[Collins 2008 - Content Externalism and Brute Logical Error]], noting that externalists who accept `P-93` while resisting `P-94` must contest `P-95` by treating logical norms as *ceteris paribus* laws or by embracing logical luck.
