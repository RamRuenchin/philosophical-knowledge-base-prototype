# Content Infallibility Definition (`D-02`)

## Formulation
Transparency of mental content denotes single-token authoritative self-knowledge: infallible first-person knowledge of what one is thinking within a single thought episode without comparative discrimination across distinct thought tokens.

## Explanation
This weaker, Tyler Burge-style definition limits transparency to "cogito-style" self-verifying judgments about a single thought at a time—such as thinking "I hereby think that water is wet," which automatically inherits the content of the first-order thought and cannot misidentify it. Unlike full comparative transparency ([[Comparative A Priori Transparency Definition]], `D-01`), it does not require the thinker to know from armchair reflection alone whether two separate thoughts share or differ in meaning. Under this reading, [[Mental Content Is Epistemically Transparent]] (`P-02`) sharpens into [[Single-Token Content Infallibility Holds]] (`P-17`), a modest form of self-knowledge that even critics of content externalism generally accept.

**Defines:** [[Transparency of Mental Content]] (`C-01`)

## Usage in Literature

[[Tyler Burge]] defends this cogito-style reading of authoritative self-knowledge across the anti-individualism debate (reconstructed in [[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]], [[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]], and [[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]]). Even [[Paul Boghossian]] concedes that single-token content infallibility holds (`P-17`) while maintaining that it is too weak to secure comparative transparency (`D-01`) or wide rationalizing explanation.
