# Externalism Is Compatible with Discriminatory Knowledge of Content (`P-31`)

## Formulation

Content externalism is compatible with introspective discriminatory knowledge of mental content.

## Explanation

This thesis sharpens [[Content Externalism Is Compatible with Introspective Knowledge of Content]] (`P-05`) under [[Discriminatory Knowledge of Content Definition]] (`D-26`). Here *Discriminatory Knowledge of Content* (`DKC`) is the introspective ability to tell, without checking the external world, whether two of your thoughts share the same meaning or differ—or to distinguish your actual thought from a subjectively identical Twin Earth counterpart. Almost all philosophers reject `DKC` compatibility under externalism, because a thinker who switches environments or belongs to split communities can host two different external meanings behind thoughts that feel identical from inside; Sanford Goldberg uses his Sally case—where Sally forgets which of two community-distinct senses of "pragmatist" she had in mind in a remembered premise—to show that losing `DKC` directly disrupts truth-directed reasoning even when single-thought Knowledge of Content Proper (`KC`, `P-30`) is preserved.

## Usage in Literature

In [[Goldberg 1999 - The Relevance of Discriminatory Knowledge of Content]], [[Sanford C. Goldberg]] rejects this thesis, noting that following Falvey and Owens (1994), most compatibilist anti-individualists already concede that anti-individualism is incompatible with introspective DKC while attempting to preserve [[Externalism Is Compatible with Knowledge of Content Proper]] (`P-30`). Goldberg's central contribution (via Sally's "pragmatist" case in Sally DKC Relevance Argument ([[Goldberg 1999 - The Relevance of Discriminatory Knowledge of Content#Arguments]])) is to show that this conceded loss of DKC is not epistemically benign because it directly impacts truth-directed reasoning.
