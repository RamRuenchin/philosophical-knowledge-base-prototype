# Two-Concept Content Externalism (`P-41`)

## Formulation

Both some mental states have contents that constitutively depend on external environmental factors and slow switching produces two-concept cohabitation with originary memory fixation.

## Explanation

This composite theory pairs basic content externalism (`P-40`, the view that external physical or social environments partly determine what some thoughts mean) with the "conceptual addition" account of slow switching (`D-21`). In plain terms, if someone unknowingly moves from Earth to Twin Earth and lives there long enough to pick up the local twin concept (such as Twin Earth "twater" or "Twin Pavarotti"), they do *not* lose their old Earth concept: instead, earlier memories stay anchored to their original Earth meaning (*originary memory fixation*) while new perceptions use the Twin Earth meaning, so both concepts sit side by side behind the same word (*two-concept cohabitation*). This setup drives major slow-switching puzzles—including slow-switched Peter's undetectable equivocation, slow-switched Schmidt's rational differential doubt without transparency of difference (`TDC`), and slow-switched Oscar's unreliable sameness judgments undermining transparency of sameness (`TSC`).
