# Externalism Is Compatible with Contingent-Form Rationality (`P-25`)

## Formulation

Content externalism is compatible with deductive-form rationality in which wide-content sameness across premises is contingent.

## Explanation

This thesis sharpens [[Content Externalism Is Compatible with Rational Inference]] (`P-04`) under [[Contingent-Form Rationality Definition]] (`D-13`), offering a weaker standard of rationality designed to live peacefully with content externalism (the view that external environments partly fix mental content). In plain terms, it says your inference is epistemically justified whenever it follows a valid deductive pattern that *would* guarantee validity apart from undetectable externalist quirks—even though whether two occurrences of a term actually share the same externally fixed ("wide") meaning is a contingent worldly fact. Stephen Schiffer proposes this fallback so that slow-switched Peter (who unknowingly moves from Earth to Twin Earth and mixes an Earth memory with a Twin Earth perception) counts as epistemically justified without needing to posit special "narrow" mental contents inside the head.

## Usage in Literature

In [[Schiffer 1992 - Boghossian on Externalism and Inference]], [[Stephen Schiffer]] endorses this thesis as a fallback response to [[Paul Boghossian]]'s inference to the best explanation for narrow content: even if undetectable externalist contingencies could threaten validity, Peter's epistemic justification is explained by his instantiating a valid deductive form modulo such contingencies, without needing narrow content. In [[Boghossian 1992 - Reply to Schiffer]], Boghossian presses the non-identity slow-switching case to maintain that externalist causal fixation still undermines a priori transparency of validity rather than leaving Schiffer's univocal-intent defense unchallenged.
