# Contextualist Content Externalism (`P-118`)

## Formulation

Both some mental states have contents that constitutively depend on external environmental factors and the semantic contents assigned to a thinker's standing thoughts vary with context across switch episodes.

## Explanation

Bridging [[Content Externalism]] (`S-01`) and [[Metasemantic Contextualism]] (`S-03`), this theory keeps mental content anchored to external physical and social environments while denying that a stored memory or standing belief retains one frozen, context-independent content across environmental switches. Instead, which external content is attributed to a thinker's standing thoughts varies across contexts—either via the thinker's own shifting commitment weights in a single deliberative context (Henry Jackman, `P-32`) or via an outside interpreter's shifting conversational and explanatory interests (Robert Stalnaker, `P-33`)—allowing contextualist externalists to resist slow-switching equivocation (`P-47`).

### Led To By / Challenged By

- Defeasibly challenged by [[Two-Concept Content Externalism]] (`P-41`) via originary memory fixation (`D-21`), which locks pre-switch memories to their original acquisition environment rather than current deliberative context ([[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]], [[Jackman 2015 - Externalism, metasemantic contextualism, and self-knowledge]]).

## Usage in Literature

[[Robert Stalnaker]] (as reconstructed in [[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]] and [[Jackman 2015 - Externalism, metasemantic contextualism, and self-knowledge]]) and [[Henry Jackman]] ([[Jackman 2015 - Externalism, metasemantic contextualism, and self-knowledge]]) both endorse contextualist content externalism to dissolve slow-switching equivocation, though Jackman sharply separates his speaker-weighting formulation (`P-32`) from Stalnaker's radical interpreter-relative formulation (`P-33`). [[Paul Boghossian]] rejects contextualist externalism in [[Boghossian 2011 - The transparency of mental content revisited]], arguing that Stalnaker's tacit identity presuppositions are circular and threaten attributional regress without [[Intentional Realism]] (`P-42`).
