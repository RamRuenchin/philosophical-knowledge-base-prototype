# Competence-Based Normal-Environment Rationality Definition (`D-15`)

## Formulation
Rationality and inferential normativity denotes exercising a reasoning competence that is reliably validity-conducive in normal environments.

## Explanation
On Mikkel Gerken's competence-based standard, an inference is rational when the thinker exercises a cognitive skill that reliably produces valid deductions in normal environments, even if an abnormal trick like undetectable slow switching makes a particular step equivocate. This charts a middle course between demanding actual validity on every single occasion ([[A Priori Logical-Conformity Rationality Definition]], `D-12`; [[Unrestricted Validity Requirement Definition]], `D-37`) and settling for mere subjective blamelessness ([[Blameless Identity-Sensitivity Rationality Definition]], `D-14`; [[Deontological Blamelessness Warrant Definition]], `D-39`), which Gerken rejects because blameless Pete committing the gambler's fallacy still lacks a reliable reasoning competence. Under this reading, [[Content Externalism Is Compatible with Rational Inference]] (`P-04`) sharpens into [[Externalism Is Compatible with Competence-Based Rationality]] (`P-27`).

**Defines:** [[Rationality and Inferential Normativity]] (`C-03`)

## Usage in Literature

[[Mikkel Gerken]] develops and endorses this sharpening in [[Gerken 2011 - Conceptual Equivocation and Warrant by Reasoning]] to resolve his Validity Argument against anti-individualism without collapsing into epistemic deontologism (which he refutes via Pete's gambler's fallacy).
