# Amalgam File-Fusion Memory Definition (`D-24`)

## Formulation
Content-preserving memory across slow switching denotes amalgam file-fusion memory in which Earth and Twin-Earth concepts merge into a single compound concept.

## Explanation
Inspired by exemplar-based views of concepts (such as Jane Heal's), this model proposes that after a slow switch between Earth and Twin Earth, the thinker's old Earth concept and new Twin-Earth concept fuse into one broad compound or disjunctive concept covering examples from both planets. While merging the two concepts aims to stop slow-switched inferences from equivocating, critics like Jessica Brown and John Collins reject it because it warps the truth conditions of pre-switch memories and fails to help with non-switching opacity puzzles—such as Brown's non-switched Frances, who learns "einsteinium" and "fermium" under identical textbook descriptions without ever changing planets.

## Usage in Literature

[[Jessica Brown]] ([[Brown 2004 - Anti-Individualism and Knowledge]]) and [[John M. Collins]] ([[Collins 2008 - Content Externalism and Brute Logical Error]]) both examine and reject the amalgam/file-fusion strategy, arguing that it fails to generalize to non-switching cases (like Clara/Frances) and yields implausible truth conditions for pre-switch memories.
