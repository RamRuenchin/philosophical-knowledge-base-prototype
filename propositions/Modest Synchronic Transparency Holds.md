# Modest Synchronic Transparency Holds (`P-19`)

## Formulation

Modest first-person synchronic conceptual transparency holds even under content externalism.

## Explanation

Sharpening [[Mental Content Is Epistemically Transparent]] (`P-02`) under [[Modest Synchronic Transparency Definition]] (`D-04`), this thesis makes a carefully scaled-back claim about self-knowledge: at any single moment in time (*synchronically*), if you attach meanings to two expressions you are currently entertaining, you can tell purely from reflection whether those two meanings are the same or different—even under content externalism (where your environment partly fixes meaning). Crucially, it abandons two stronger Cartesian demands: it does not require you to infallibly reidentify concepts across long stretches of time (avoiding slow-switching puzzles), nor does it claim you can prove from inside alone that your concepts actually refer to real objects in the world.

## Usage in Literature

In [[Szubka 2000 - Meaning Rationalism, A Priori, and Transparency of Content]], [[Tadeusz Szubka]] endorses this moderated Dummettian transparency thesis as a viable, indispensable first-person constraint that survives externalist critiques of Strong Meaning Rationalism (`P-20`). By contrast, [[Ruth Garrett Millikan]] (as reconstructed in [[Szubka 2000 - Meaning Rationalism, A Priori, and Transparency of Content]]) rejects even modest synchronic transparency because she accords strict priority to the third-person biosemantic perspective.
