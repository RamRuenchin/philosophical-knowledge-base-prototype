# Content Externalism Has a Superior Repertoire of Replies on Peter (`P-57`)

## Formulation

Non-Millian content externalism has a strictly superior repertoire of replies to slow-switching puzzles compared to content internalism.

## Explanation

This thesis expresses Manuel Pérez Otero's "reversal" of Boghossian's slow-switching argument against [[Content Externalism]] (the view that mental content partly depends on one's external environment). Examining slow-switched Peter—who unknowingly moves from Earth to Twin Earth and mixes an Earth memory about Pavarotti with a Twin-Earth perception of Twin Pavarotti (`P-46`)—Otero argues that *non-Millian* content externalism (externalism that allows internal cognitive modes of presentation alongside external reference, rather than reducing meaning purely to bare reference) actually has a strictly better toolkit of replies than content internalism. While internalists either face counterintuitive semantics (`P-59`) or rely on internal differentiators that non-Millian externalists can also adopt (`P-60`), externalists retain additional options such as anaphoric memory and logical luck.

## Usage in Literature

[[Manuel Pérez Otero]] defends `P-57` in [[Otero 2012 - Boghossian's Inference Argument against Content Externalism Reversed]] as the conclusion of `ARG-07`, concluding that Peter's singular-term inference inadvertently supplies evidence favoring [[Content Externalism]] over internalism. [[Paul Boghossian]] rejects `P-57` in [[Boghossian 2014 - Reply to Otero's Boghossian's Inference Argument against Content Externalism Reversed]], conceding theory-neutrality (`P-58`) but replying that his original argument covered general terms like "water" (not just proper names) and that narrow syntactic validity (`P-61`) gives internalists an explanation of Peter's rational preparedness at least as viable as externalist anaphoric or logical-luck replies.
