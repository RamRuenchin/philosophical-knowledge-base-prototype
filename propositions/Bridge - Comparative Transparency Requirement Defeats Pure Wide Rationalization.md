# Bridge - Comparative Transparency Requirement Defeats Pure Wide Rationalization (`P-65`)

## Formulation

If both (rational inference requires comparative a priori transparency of mental content) and (it is not the case that both every thinker can know a priori, for every two same-content thought tokens of that thinker, that they have the same content, and every thinker can know a priori, for every two different-content thought tokens of that thinker, that they differ in content), then it is not the case that wide content alone suffices for rationalizing psychological explanation.

## Explanation

*Comparative a priori transparency* (`P-12`) combines two reflective capacities: knowing *a priori* (by reflection alone) whenever two of one's thoughts share the same content (*Transparency of Sameness of Content*, TSC) and knowing *a priori* whenever they differ in content (*Transparency of Difference of Content*, TDC). This bridge states that if genuine rational inference requires comparative a priori transparency (`P-13`), yet environmentally determined ("wide") contents fail to satisfy it—as shown by slow-switching and co-reference puzzles—then wide content alone cannot suffice for rationalizing psychological explanation (`P-54`), which explains why an agent's reasoning makes sense from their own point of view.

## Usage in Literature

[[Paul Boghossian]] endorses `P-65` across [[Boghossian 1992 - Externalism and Inference]] and [[Boghossian 1994 - The Transparency of Mental Content]] as the crucial bridge in `ARG-05`: once comparative a priori transparency (`P-13`) is required for rational inference and psychological rationalization (mirroring why non-transparent *de re* beliefs are excluded from rational psychology), the failure of full comparative transparency under externalism (`~P-12`) entails that pure wide content cannot sustain rationalizing explanation (`P-54`), forcing appeal to a non-wide intentional level (`P-55`).
