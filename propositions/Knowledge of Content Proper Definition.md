# Knowledge of Content Proper Definition (`D-25`)

## Formulation
Knowledge of content denotes authoritative first-person knowledge of the content of an individual thought.

## Explanation
Sanford Goldberg introduces the label "Knowledge of Content Proper" (KC) for a thinker's authoritative first-person grasp of what a single thought means, separating it from the comparative ability to tell whether two thoughts have the same or different meanings (Discriminatory Knowledge of Content, or DKC, [[Discriminatory Knowledge of Content Definition]], `D-26`). Even externalists like Tyler Burge easily defend KC, which explains why compatibilists and critics talk past each other unless they distinguish single-thought knowledge from cross-thought discrimination. Under this reading, [[Content Externalism Is Compatible with Introspective Knowledge of Content]] (`P-05`) sharpens into [[Externalism Is Compatible with Knowledge of Content Proper]] (`P-30`).

**Defines:** [[Knowledge of Content]] (`C-06`)

## Usage in Literature

[[Tyler Burge]]'s compatibilist self-knowledge defense is captured under `D-25` in [[Goldberg 1999 - The Relevance of Discriminatory Knowledge of Content]], where [[Sanford C. Goldberg]] grants that anti-individualism is compatible with KC (`P-30`) while arguing that rational reasoning additionally depends on DKC (`D-26`).
