# Narrow-Syntactic APLA Holds (`P-23`)

## Formulation

Every thinker possesses a priori sensitivity to narrow syntactic logical form that explains that thinker's rational preparedness to draw inferences.

## Explanation

This thesis sharpens [[Thinkers Possess A Priori Logical Abilities]] (`P-03`) under [[Narrow-Syntactic APLA Definition]] (`D-11`), where `APLA` stands for *a priori logical abilities*. Instead of claiming that you can tell from reflection alone whether the externally fixed ("wide") meanings in your argument match (`P-21`), it says you have direct inward sensitivity to the *narrow syntactic form* of your thoughts—the internal mental symbols or psychological modes inside your head. Paul Boghossian uses this fallback to explain why slow-switched Peter (who unknowingly moved from Earth to Twin Earth and mixes a pre-switch Earth memory with a post-switch Twin Earth perception) is rationally prepared to draw his conclusion: at the narrow internal level, his inference genuinely fits a valid syntactic pattern even though its wide external referents silently split.

In [[Boghossian 2014 - Reply to Otero's Boghossian's Inference Argument against Content Externalism Reversed]], [[Paul Boghossian]] endorses this narrow-syntactic reading to answer [[Manuel Pérez Otero]]'s comparative reversal: even if Peter's inference equivocates at the wide externalist level, its validity under narrow syntactic individuation (`P-61`) explains why Peter is rationally prepared to draw it, keeping internalism competitive with externalist anaphoric and logical-luck accounts.
