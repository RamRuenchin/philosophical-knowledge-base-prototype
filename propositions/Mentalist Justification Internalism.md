# Mentalist Justification Internalism (`P-120`)

## Formulation

Necessarily, any two thinkers who are identical in all of their internal non-relational mental states are identically justified in their beliefs, independently of whether those internal states are reflectively accessible.

## Explanation

Adapted from Earl Conee and Richard Feldman's *mentalism*, this non-accessibilist branch of [[Justification Internalism]] (`S-02`) holds that epistemic justification supervenes strictly on what is internal to the thinker's mind—experiences, beliefs, memories, and first-order rational dispositions—without requiring that the thinker be able to articulate or recognize those justifying factors through higher-order reflection ([[Accessibilist Justification Internalism]], `P-119`). Separating mentalism from accessibilism allows internalists like Brie Gertler to reject demanding reflective-access requirements while defending narrow internalism about rational sensitivity and cognitive value.

## Inferential Relations
### Leads To
- Does not entail [[Accessibilist Justification Internalism]] (`P-119`), because supervenience on internal mental states permits justifying factors that are not reflectively accessible ([[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism]]).

### Led To By / Challenged By
- Defeasibly entailed by [[Accessibilist Justification Internalism]] (`P-119`) insofar as reflectively accessible justification-conferring factors are internal mental states ([[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism]], [[Morvarid 2021 - A new argument for the incompatibility of content externalism with justification internalism]]).

## Usage in Literature

[[Brie Gertler]] introduces Feldman and Conee's distinction between mentalism (`P-120`) and accessibilism (`P-119`) at the outset of [[Gertler 2015 - Internalism, Externalism, and Accessibilism]] to argue that conflating internalism with accessibilism has distorted both epistemology and philosophy of mind; she rejects accessibilism (`P-119`) and reflective explicatory access (`P-15`) while adapting the mentalist supervenience model to defend narrow Fregean [[Cognitive Value]] grounded in first-order rational dispositions. [[Mahmoud Morvarid]] ([[Morvarid 2021 - A new argument for the incompatibility of content externalism with justification internalism]]) distinguishes accessibilist internalism (`P-119`, specifically `JFA` and `SA`) from purely mentalist supervenience when targeting accessibilist formulations in his slow-switching incompatibility argument.
